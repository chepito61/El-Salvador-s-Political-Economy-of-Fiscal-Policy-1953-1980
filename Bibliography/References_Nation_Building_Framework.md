# References: Literature for the Nation-Building Reframing

**Working note — 13 November 2026.** These are the full references for the literature proposed in the *Note on a Military Regime Interested in Nation Building* (section 3). Two page ranges are given from memory and are marked "to confirm".

---

## Huntington: the military as modernizers and political decay

Huntington, Samuel P. 1968. *Political Order in Changing Societies*. New Haven and London: Yale University Press. xiii + 488 pp.

- Relevant chapters:
  - chapter 1, "Political Order and Political Decay";
  - chapter 4, "Praetorianism and Political Decay" (about pp. 192–263; to confirm).
- **Use in the study:** the military as modernizers, and political decay when participation outruns institutions.

## O'Donnell: modernization and authoritarian outcomes

O'Donnell, Guillermo A. 1973. *Modernization and Bureaucratic-Authoritarianism: Studies in South American Politics*. Politics of Modernization Series No. 9. Berkeley: Institute of International Studies, University of California. 219 pp.

- **Use in the study:** modernization producing authoritarian outcomes. El Salvador is not a bureaucratic-authoritarian case in his sense, but the contrast is useful.

## Stepan: the "new professionalism" of internal security and development

Stepan, Alfred. 1973. "The New Professionalism of Internal Warfare and Military Role Expansion." In Alfred Stepan (ed.), *Authoritarian Brazil: Origins, Policies, and Future*, pp. 47–65 (to confirm). New Haven: Yale University Press.

- **Companion work:** Stepan, Alfred. 1971. *The Military in Politics: Changing Patterns in Brazil*. Princeton: Princeton University Press.
- **Use in the study:** the doctrine that tied national development to internal security and counter-insurgency in the 1960s.

## Besley and Persson: fiscal capacity

Besley, Timothy, and Torsten Persson. 2011. *Pillars of Prosperity: The Political Economics of Development Clusters*. Princeton: Princeton University Press.

- **Journal version:** Besley, Timothy, and Torsten Persson. 2009. "The Origins of State Capacity: Property Rights, Taxation, and Politics." *American Economic Review* 99(4): 1218–1244.
- **Use in the study:** fiscal capacity as the missing link in a State that spends to build the nation but cannot tax.

## Schneider: tax regimes in Central America

Schneider, Aaron. 2012. *State-Building and Tax Regimes in Central America*. New York: Cambridge University Press. 260 pp.

- This is the book on taxation and state formation in Central America that the author recalled. Schneider taught at Tulane; he is now listed at the University of Denver.
- **Use in the study:** tax regimes, elite cohesion and the coalitions that shaped them.
