# Unit-Level Review of the Pillars: Pilot Year 1977

**Working note for discussion — 12 November 2026.** This note is the first step of the validation agreed today. It reviews Interior, the Presidency and Agriculture unit by unit, plus the coalition items found in other ministries, for one pilot year. It then rebuilds the three pillars for that year with the coalition pillar balanced (spending plus tax spending).

**Pilot year: 1977.** That year's units are reported in Estado IV-2 of the 1978 Memoria (*Estado de gastos con especificación de unidades de organización y clases generales del Presupuesto General de 1977*). The 1977 budget was executed half under Molina and half under Romero, who took office on 1 July. Every figure below was read on the page image.

**Basis.** Estado IV-2 records budget execution by unit. Its grand total for 1977 is ₡1,010.54M, which differs from the credits used of the fiscal workbook (₡1,241.9M). Shares in this note are of the IV-2 total, so they are internally consistent but not identical to the workbook's pillar shares.

---

## 1. Interior: no police, one coalition item

Interior spent ₡33.80M in 1977. Its units are civil:

- central administration;
- regional development and governors' offices;
- the national printing office and the Diario Oficial;
- the postal service;
- communications operations;
- migration control, which includes an "Investigaciones" sub-unit of ₡0.14M;
- transfers to municipalities;
- the construction of the ministry's building (₡4.23M).

**No police, paramilitary or ORDEN unit appears in Interior in 1977.** The security apparatus is not hidden in this ministry's budget.

**A coalition item.** Interior paid **₡6.96M "to the municipalities of the Republic to compensate for the suppression of taxes on the cultivation of coffee"**. Municipal taxes on coffee had been abolished, and the central government replaced the lost revenue from the general budget. In effect, all taxpayers paid the coffee growers' former local taxes. The 1950 coffee decree (D. 836) already assigned part of the coffee tax to coffee municipalities; the date of the suppression and of this compensation is to be checked in the Diario Oficial.

- **The opaque lump sums.** ₡19.38M of "non-personal services" in the first two units carries no further detail. It is almost 2% of all spending, and almost a quarter of the Defence-plus-Public-Security budget. Its content is unknown. Candidates include:
  - discretionary political spending;
  - intelligence;
  - the financing of the rural network.
  - The Diario Oficial (budget law and any regulations) and the Presidency's own accounts are the place to look.
- **Fomento y Cooperación Comunal** (₡8.57M) is the community-development programme run from the Presidency. It is development spending, but delivered directly by the President's office in the countryside, where ORDEN also operated. Whether it served legitimacy, patronage or both is a question for the sources.

## 3. Agriculture: three different things

Agricultura y Ganadería spent ₡95.63M in 1977. Its units fall into three groups.

| Group | Units (examples) | ₡M |
|:--|:--|--:|
| **Coalition (export crops)** | Transfer to the Instituto Salvadoreño de Investigaciones del Café (ISIC) 3.42; promotion of agro-industrial crops 0.82 | **4.24** |
| **Food and agrarian** | Transfer to the Instituto Regulador de Abastecimientos (IRA, food prices) 13.16; transfer to ISTA (agrarian transformation) 5.33; promotion of basic grains 3.04 | **21.53** |
| **General agricultural services** | Administration, planning, plant and animal health (*defensa agropecuaria*), statistics, livestock, natural resources, fisheries, technology and extension, irrigation, agricultural schools | about 69.9 |

*The transfers to ISIC, ISTA and IRA add to the printed total of the transfer line (₡21,910,376.07). The "general" group is the remainder of the ministry's total and is shown approximately.*

**Only a small part of Agriculture is coalition spending.** Using the whole ministry as "agro-export support" (as the provisional pillar did) overstates the coalition pillar on the budget side. The rise of Agriculture in 1974–77 was driven partly by IRA and ISTA. IRA subsidized food for consumers (relevant to H5); ISTA was the agrarian reform the coalition defeated in 1976.

## 6. What the pilot shows

1. **Security is not hidden in Interior.** In 1977 the police were in Seguridad Pública and Defensa, as assumed. The security pillar holds, but the Presidency's ₡19.4M of opaque lump sums is the main uncertainty. If even half of it served security, the security pillar would rise from 8.3% to about 9.3%.
2. **The coalition is served on the tax side.** On the budget, the coalition received 1.7% of spending in 1977, much less than the provisional "Agriculture" pillar suggested. With untaxed cotton alone, it reaches 2.7–6.8% of total effort. The coffee and industrial exemptions are still missing, so the true figure is higher.
3. **Some coalition spending is hidden in other ministries.** The best example is Interior's ₡6.96M to replace the municipal coffee taxes, a transfer from general taxpayers to the coffee municipalities, booked as municipal finance.
4. **The "agro-export overtakes security" result of 1974–77 does not survive as stated.** It came from counting all of Agriculture, including IRA and ISTA. On a unit basis, the coalition's budget share is small. Its weight lies in tax spending.
