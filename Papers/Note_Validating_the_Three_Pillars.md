# Validating the Three Pillars: What to Check, Where, and How

**Working note for discussion — 12 November 2026.** The author's point: the three pillars (development, security, agro-export support) are built from ministry totals in the Memorias. Before they carry the argument of the paper, they need validation with micro data from the Diario Oficial and other sources. This note sets out where the current measure can mislead, the tests that would validate or correct it, the sources, and a sequence.

---

## 1. How the pillars are measured now, and why that is not enough

**Current measure.** Ministry (*ramo*) totals from the Memorias, on a credits-used basis where available:

- **Development** = Educación + Salud Pública + Obras Públicas;
- **Security** = Defensa + Seguridad Pública;
- **Agro-export support** = Agricultura y Ganadería.

**Where it can mislead:**

| Risk | Example | Direction of error |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Security spending outside Defensa and Seguridad Pública | Police or paramilitary units in Interior; the rural network ORDEN; Presidency lump sums (*partidas* for "security" or "special expenses"); military pensions in Clases Pasivas; war-bond service in Deuda Pública | Security understated |
| Security inside other lines of Defensa | Guardia Nacional, Policía de Hacienda and Policía Nacional moved between Defensa and Seguridad Pública across years | Shifts within the pillar; breaks in the series |
| Spending outside the ordinary budget | Extraordinary budgets and special decrees (*créditos extraordinarios*, *refuerzos*); arms purchases financed by loans or special bonds (1969); military aid in kind | Security understated, especially in crisis years |
| Development lines that serve the coalition | Roads to coffee and cotton zones; the port of Acajutla; electricity for industry | Development overstated as "legitimacy"; agro-export support understated |
| Agro-export support through the tax system and credit, not the budget | The coffee income-tax exemption (1950); untaxed cotton; credit to the coffee sector (the ₡30M General Fund loan to the Compañía Salvadoreña de Café, 1975) | Agro-export support badly understated |
| Autonomous institutions off-budget | CEL, IRA, ISTA, ISSS, the University (only the subsidy is in the budget) | All pillars understated; agrarian and food programmes especially |
| Basis changes | Credits used against gastos against final execution | Artificial jumps (marked in the workbook) |
| Nominal shares | Shares can rise while real spending per teacher or per student falls | Development's "legitimacy" overstated in inflationary years (1974–79) |

The 1950s results (security 13–16%, development 38–46%) are probably robust in direction. The 1970s results, especially the security doubling of 1978–79 and the agro-export overtaking of 1974–77, are the ones most exposed to these errors.

## 2. Validation tests, pillar by pillar

### 2.1 Security

1. **Unit-level reconstruction.** Use the Ley de Salarios (posts and salaries by unit, published in the Diario Oficial with each budget) and the Memorias' unit tables (Estado N° 5 / IV-2). Count every armed or police unit wherever it sits:
   - Fuerza Armada;
   - Guardia Nacional;
   - Policía Nacional;
   - Policía de Hacienda;
   - Policía de Aduanas;
   - any security units in Interior or the Presidency.
2. **Posts, not only colones.** The number of security posts (soldiers, guards, police) against teachers and health workers, from the Ley de Salarios. Posts are less affected by inflation and basis changes.
3. **Off-budget and extraordinary items:**
   - decrees of extraordinary credits and budget transfers to Defensa (Diario Oficial);
   - the 1969 war bonds and arms purchases;
   - military assistance from the United States, from the US foreign-aid record (the "Greenbook") and from arms-transfer registers (SIPRI, from 1950).
4. **ORDEN.** Its founding decree or legal status, and any budget line that funded it (Interior, Defensa or the Presidency).
5. **Military pensions** (Clases Pasivas, military part) as a memo line.

### 2.2 Development

1. **Teachers and the University:**
   - teaching posts and salary scales by year (Ley de Salarios; the teachers' salary laws, including the scale demanded in 1971);
   - the University subsidy (Memoria unit lines; University budgets).
2. **Real terms per user.** Education spending per enrolled student, and teachers' real salaries, once a price index before 1974 is found. Enrolment is in the Education note (1967–74) and the Anuarios Estadísticos.
3. **Public works by purpose.** Separate roads and works that mainly serve export zones (coffee and cotton roads, the Litoral highway, Acajutla) from schools, water, health posts and urban works. Sources: the loan register; the extraordinary budgets; the project lists in the Memorias.

### 2.3 Agro-export support

1. **The tax side.** Estimate the main tax expenditures for the coalition:
   - the coffee income-tax exemption (1950);
   - cotton's freedom from export duty;
   - industrial and export promotion exemptions (1952, 1961, 1970, 1974).
   - Sources: the laws (Diario Oficial); CEPAL and World Bank estimates (the 1979 World Bank report has tax-expenditure material).
2. **The credit side:**
   - General Fund loans and guarantees to the coffee sector (Compañía Salvadoreña de Café; INCAFE from 1979);
   - BCR credit lines to agro-export (BCR reports).
3. **The budget side, unit by unit:** Agricultura's units for coffee and cotton research, extension and credit, against its units for basic grains, colonization and the agrarian reform (ISTA from 1975).

## 4. Method and sequence

1. **Benchmark years first, not all years.** Validate eight years that mark the turns:
   - 1954 (the boom);
   - 1959 (the 1950s adjustment);
   - 1965 (Rivera's peak);
   - 1969 (the war);
   - 1971 (the education peak and the strike);
   - 1976 (the security low and the agrarian transformation);
   - 1978 (the bust);
   - 1979 (the security peak).

   For each year, read the Ley de Presupuesto and the Ley de Salarios in the Diario Oficial, and the extraordinary-credit decrees.
2. **Two measures side by side.** Keep the current "ministry pillars", and build "adjusted pillars" that reassign units by function and add the off-budget and tax-side items. Report both, and how far they differ.
3. **A posts-based measure.** Security posts against teaching and health posts, from the Ley de Salarios, as a check that does not depend on prices or basis.
4. **Decide on the result.** If the adjusted pillars confirm the turning points (1950s shift; 1971 education peak; 1974–77 agro-export overtaking; 1978–79 security doubling), the paper uses them with confidence. If not, the paper reports the correction.

---

## 5. What this means for the paper now

- The preliminary paper presents the pillars as **ministry-based and provisional**, with the risks in section 1 stated plainly. The validation programme goes in the research agenda.
- **Claims to word carefully until validated:**
  - "security doubled in 1978–79";
  - "agro-export support overtook security in 1974–77";
  - any statement that the coalition was served "through the tax system rather than the budget".
- The 1950s benchmark (Pleitez's 1827–1948 averages) stays as an order of magnitude.
