It replaces the working inventory *Bibliography/Data_Gaps_and_Sources_1948_1980.md* (November 2026), which it updates with the gaps found in the 1960 notes, the new sections of the briefing on investment, debt and monetary policy, and the literature review. Priorities: **1** limits a main conclusion; **2** needed for a hypothesis test; **3** useful detail.

# 1. Purpose and how to use this document

**The question.** Did the fiscal and monetary management of a small, open, agro-exporting and dependent economy contribute to the political polarization that led to the civil war? The Memorias de Hacienda give us, year by year, what the State collected, spent, invested and borrowed (1952–1979). What they do not give us falls into five groups:

1. **The years and statements the Memorias do not cover** (1948–1951, 1980; statements missing from the scans).
2. **The laws behind the numbers**, which are in the Diario Oficial: who was paid, at what scale, who was exempted, which decrees moved money.
3. **The economy around the budget**: GDP, prices, coffee volumes, money, reserves and credit, which are in the Central Reserve Bank (BCR), CEPAL and the IMF.
4. **The State outside the budget**: the autonomous institutions, guarantees, extraordinary budgets, paramilitary networks.
5. **The social and political record** to set against the fiscal one: wages, employment, protest and repression.

**The two assistants' tasks.**

- **Research Assistant A (database and econometrics)** extends and cleans the database, builds the real and GDP-based series, and runs the econometric agenda of section 13 of the briefing. A's first needs are the macro series (section 4 below), the economic classification of spending (section 3.2) and the investment and debt series (section 6).
- **Research Assistant B (archives)** works in the Diario Oficial, the BCR, the Biblioteca de Hacienda and the online archives (sections 2, 3, 5, 7 and 8 below), and verifies the facts we have taken from secondary sources (section 9).

Each item gives the hypothesis it serves. The hypotheses are listed in the briefing, section 12: H1–H5 (education, teachers' wages, investment, monetary policy, food subsidies), Tracks A–D (tax bargain, Honduran safety valve, protection bargain, reforms diluted), the four political-economy hypotheses, and I1–I4 (investment and debt).

**Rules for new data.** Every figure is recorded with its source and page, read on the original image, checked against printed totals, and appended to the data files without overwriting earlier entries (*Tools/Workflow_and_Conventions.md*). Secondary-source figures are labelled as such until confirmed in a primary source.

# 12. Suggested order of work

**Research Assistant A (database and econometrics)**

1. Weeks 1–2: learn the database and conventions; reproduce Annex A of the briefing from the workbook.
2. Weeks 2–4: build GDP, CPI and population series from CEPAL, the World Bank appendices and Acevedo; Pink Sheet and Grilli–Yang coffee prices.

**Research Assistant B (archives)**

1. Weeks 1–2: online sources: IMF Archives (consultation reports, the 1960 stand-by), CEPAL, World Bank and IDB project documents, USAID DEC.
2. Weeks 2–4: one request to the Biblioteca de Hacienda (UAIP) for Memorias 1948–51 and 1980–81 and the statements missing from the scans.
