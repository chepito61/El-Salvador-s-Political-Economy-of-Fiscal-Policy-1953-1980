# El Salvador: Fiscal Diagnostic and Vulnerability Assessment, 1978

**Technical Note — The Coffee Bust: Revenue Collapse, Security Protected, Arrears Exposed (November 2026)**

> **About this note.** This is the fiscal diagnostic for 1978. It follows the structure of the diagnostic series for 1952–1977.
>
> **Sources.** The *Informe Complementario Constitucional* 1978:
> - the analytical narrative (Cuadros 1–24);
> - Estados I-4 and II-1 (revenue, 1974–1978);
> - Estado III-9 (public debt by instrument, 1974–1978);
> - Estado IV-2 (spending by unit, **1977 budget liquidated at end-1978**).
>
> Ministry comparisons are on credits used (Cuadro 9). Every figure was read on the page image and checked by arithmetic.
>
> **Revised series.** MH 1978 revises the public debt for 1974–1977 (Cuadro 24) and GDP, hence the tax burden, for 1975–1977 (Cuadro 12). This note uses the revised figures throughout; the 1976 and 1977 notes carry dated corrections.
>
> **Pillars.** Development = education + health + public works. Security = Defensa + Ramo de Seguridad Pública.

---

## Executive Summary

**The fiscal position is assessed as "revenue collapse with the coffee price; deficit of a fifth of revenue; security protected, education and investment cut; hidden arrears exposed; debt above threshold; very high political vulnerability".**

- **Revenue.** Current revenue −13.1% to ₡1,026.9M.
  - Coffee duty ₡126.9M (−69.6%), **12.4% of revenue** (35.3% in 1977).
  - Income tax ₡201.8M (+22.9%); company income tax ₡77.9M (+39.2%).
  - New agricultural land tax ₡10.1M.
  - Tax / GDP 12.7% (Memoria), after 15.9% in 1977 (revised).
- **Spending.** Credits used +7.5% to ₡1,334.6M; gastos +19.6% to ₡1,095.0M; commitments −26.6%.
  - Defensa +37.1%; Seguridad Pública +18.6%; Economía +94.6% (San Lorenzo dam); health +12.8%; public works +2.2%; **education −0.7%.**
  - Remunerations +17.8% (about +4.0% real); ₡60M of overdue contributions paid to the Social Security Institute with bonds.
- **Balance.** Commitment basis −₡307.7M (−30.0%). **Underlying −₡190.4M (−18.5%),** the worst of the 1968–1978 series; −12.7% excluding the Social Security arrears.
- **Liquidity.** Treasury cash ₡90.1M (−₡47.1M); **Central Bank advances ₡8.0M;** Superávit Financiero ₡10.7M, still including ₡235M of unsold bonds (about −₡224M without them).
- **Debt.** Direct debt +37.0% to ₡638.7M (**62.2% of revenue**); guaranteed debt +31.1% to ₡605.8M; national public debt ₡1,306.1M (+30.4%).
- **Composition (credits used):** development 47.5%; **security 9.2%** (7.5% in 1977).

Currency: US$1.00 = ₡2.50.

- **The direct-debt threshold was breached,** by revenue falling and borrowing rising together. Three-quarters of the rise in direct debt (₡172.4M) was external (+₡132.8M); internal bonds rose ₡39.7M, almost all the Social Security issue (₡58.0M outstanding).
- **The revised series changes the reading of 1976–77.** The Jiboa sugar mill (₡75.5M at end-1976) was financed by external bank credits to INSAFI that the State did not guarantee (Wells Fargo, ₡40.3M; Export Development Corporation of Canada, ₡37.5M), not by guaranteed internal bonds. Revised guaranteed debt **rose** 25% in 1977 (₡370.5M to ₡462.0M), and guaranteed internal bonds rose 17%. **The 1977 claim that Treasury lending replaced guaranteed bond issues is not supported by the revised series.**
- **Guarantees rose another 31% in 1978,** for CEL (San Lorenzo), CEPA (Cuscatlán airport), ISTA, ANDA, FIGAPE and FEDECRÉDITO. External debt rose ₡143.4M, of which about ₡109M for San Lorenzo and the airport.
- **External debt service was still small:** 3.7% of exports in 1978, the highest of 1974–78 (Memoria, p. 45).

## 9. Assessment

**Overall: revenue collapse with the coffee price; deficit of a fifth of revenue; security protected, education and investment cut; hidden arrears exposed; debt above threshold; very high political vulnerability.**

- **The bust arrived exactly as the 1977 diagnostic warned.** One duty on one product had carried a third of revenue; when the price fell 30%, the duty fell 70% and revenue 13%.
- **Spending did not adjust; it was reallocated.** Credits used still rose 7.5%. The cuts fell on new capital commitments (−28%), real investment and education; security rose 32% and reached its highest share of the decade.
- **The boom's surplus was partly an accounting result.** The State had not paid ₡60M of Social Security contributions, and the paper surplus still counted ₡235M of unsold bonds. Without them, the General Fund was about ₡224M in deficit.
- **Financing moved back to the Central Bank, foreign lenders and the State's own institutions.** Direct debt rose 37% and crossed 60% of revenue; guarantees rose 31%.
- **The tax bargain moved at the bottom of the cycle.** Company income tax (+39%) and a new land tax (₡10.1M) replaced about a tenth of the coffee duty lost.
- **The fiscal bust coincided with political closure.** The Public Order law, the IACHR's findings and the guerrilla kidnappings set the political context in which security was protected and education cut.
