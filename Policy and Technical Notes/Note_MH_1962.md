# El Salvador: Fiscal Year 1962 — Authoritarian Reformism's First Budget: Taxes, Central Bank Credit and the Alliance for Progress

**Working Note — Evidence from the Informe Complementario Constitucional (November 2026)**

> **About this note.** Working note on the 1962 Memoria, in the format of the 1952–1961 series, with a final section that reads the year against the project's hypotheses (H1–H5) and against the expectation set out after the 1961 note: that the 1962 budget, the Directorio's own, would show "a shift away from the University and toward security and investment". Every figure was read on the page image and checked by arithmetic. Political periods follow Roberto Turcios's terms: **democratic transition** (the Junta, October 1960–January 1961) and **authoritarian reformism** (the Directorio Cívico Militar and its successors; *Rebelión*, pp. 325–326). The IMF-style diagnostic is in the companion *Technical Note 1962*.

---

## Political Context

The 1962 budget was decreed by the Directorio Cívico Militar on 23 December 1961 (Decreto N° 482, Diario Oficial N° 237). It was executed by provisional president Eusebio Rodolfo Cordón Cea (from 25 January 1962) and, from 1 July 1962, by President Julio Adalberto Rivera (PCN), a former member of the Directorio. It is therefore the first clean expression of authoritarian reformism. The Minister of Finance, Alvaro Marino, signed the Informe on 28 February 1963; the statements were signed by René López Bertrand, Director de Contabilidad Central.

## 1. Revenue Structure and Performance

### 1.1 The revenue recovery

Rentas were **₡168,603,688.46**, up ₡10,518,548.84 (**+6.7%**) from 1961. With *otros ingresos* (₡3,074,290.71, mostly refunds of earlier spending), total income was ₡171,677,979.17, ₡11.68M (6.4%) below the budget estimate. Import duties were again the main shortfall against the estimate (₡12.72M); the income tax exceeded it by ₡2.31M.

## 8. Preliminary Observations

1. **The income-tax reform carried the recovery.** The income tax proper rose 50%, providing three-quarters of the revenue gain while coffee duties fell for the second year.
2. **Spending was frozen, payroll was not.** Payments rose 0.2%, but the wage bill rose 6.5% and teachers' salaries 13.6%. The adjustment fell on investment and casual labour.
3. **Investment was cut again.** Ordinary capital credits fell 35% (about 45% over two years) and public works 17%; commitments carried forward fell to ₡6.2M.
4. **The Central Bank financed the Treasury.** With no surplus left, the General Fund borrowed ₡15.5M from the newly nationalized BCR and ended the year with a Déficit Financiero of ₡6.9M. Payment arrears were reduced.
5. **Security was reorganized, not enlarged.** The police were absorbed into the Ramo de Defensa; the security share was flat at 12.5%, and its equipment halved.
6. **The presidency grew a third,** with a new ₡1.06M line of *gratificaciones* at Casa Presidencial — a possible beginning of the *partidas secretas*.
7. **A second, foreign-financed investment budget opened** under the Alliance for Progress: US grants for schools, PL-480 credit for rural welfare, Eximbank and DLF loans for the airport, IDB-guaranteed loans — with counterpart and debt-service claims on the ordinary budget.

## 9. Reading 1962 Against the Hypotheses

**Testing the expectation: "away from the University, toward security and investment."**

| Expected | What 1962 shows | Verdict |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Away from the University | Educación's subsidies (likely University line) −22%; teachers' salaries +13.6% | **Partly supported** — a cut in the likely University line, not in education as a whole |
| Toward security | Security −0.7%; share 12.5%; payroll flat; equipment halved; police absorbed into Defensa | **Not in money; yes institutionally** |
| Toward investment | Ordinary capital −35%, public works −17%; foreign-financed extraordinary budgets started | **Not in the ordinary budget; yes off budget, with foreign money** |

| Hypothesis | 1962 evidence | Verdict |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **H1** Education and the University squeezed | Education protected (+2.5%, record 21.9%); likely University line −22% | **Mixed** — teachers kept, University possibly cut: the first sign of separating the two constituencies |
| **H2** Wage containment | Permanent salaries +8.4%, teachers +13.6%; casual labour −12%; Defense payroll flat | **Not for teachers; yes for casual labour** |
| **H3** Public investment cut first | Capital −35% after −15% in 1961 | **Strongly supported** |
| **H4** Monetary tightening | The BCR lent ₡15.5M to the Treasury | **The opposite for the government**; private credit needs BCR data |
| **H5** IRA food subsidies cut | IRA guaranteed credit +75%; Economía subsidies fell (₡3.07M → ₡1.30M), recipients not identified | **Not supported** |

**The story in one paragraph.** Authoritarian reformism inherited an empty treasury and a budget enlarged by the Junta. It did not reverse the education expansion; teachers' pay rose again. It adjusted instead by taxing higher incomes, cutting the ordinary budget's own investment and borrowing from the Central Bank it had just nationalized, while opening a foreign-financed investment channel under the Alliance for Progress. A plausible reading, to be tested in the following years: the regime modernized its fiscal instruments rather than squeezing the urban middle sectors — with the probable exception of the University.
