**Technical Note — Recovery and Political Rupture (revised 21 November 2026)**

> **About this note.** This is the first technical diagnostic for 1960. All figures come from the scanned *Informe Complementario Constitucional* for 1960 (signed 28 February 1961 by Minister Ernesto A. Núñez), as recorded in the revised Note_MH_1960; 1959 comparisons come from the 1959 Memoria, and the fate of 1960 commitments from the 1961 Memoria. The budget balance is reported on a commitment basis and an approximate cash basis, as in the other revised diagnostics.
>
> **Revision of 21 November 2026.** Adds the Treasury balance sheet (4.5), the monetary and external position (4.6), political and reserve risks in the vulnerability matrix and dashboard, a political-risk paragraph in the assessment, and a longer list of data gaps. No figures were changed. Political context is in the companion Note_MH_1960 (from Turcios, *Rebelión*).

> Pillars (author's definitions): development = education/culture + health + public works; security = Defensa + Seguridad Pública (the Defense-controlled apparatus); agriculture and Interior reported separately.

## Executive Summary

- **Revenue recovered, led by imports.** Revenue rose ₡13.9M (+8.9%) to ₡170.5M, ending two years of decline but remaining 10% below 1957. Import duties rose 19.7% and explained 63% of the increase; coffee export duties rose only 6%.
- **Spending held flat.** Credits used (gastos + compromisos) were ₡174.3M, 0.9% below 1959; payments (gastos) fell for the second year.
- **The deficit nearly closed.** The commitment-basis result was **−₡3.7M** (−2.2% of revenue); on an approximate cash basis the budget was **roughly balanced (+₡0.8M)**.
- **The buffer rose.** The Superávit Financiero rose from ₡18.0M to ₡20.3M, helped by ₡5.7M of cancelled prior-year reservations. Treasury cash, however, fell further, to ₡8.4M.
- **Rigidity remained high.** Personnel was 53.9% of gastos; rigid and semi-rigid spending 82.7% of gastos.
- **Composition shifted toward development.** Development ramos (education, health, public works) took 45.7% of credits used — the highest of the decade — and security (Defensa + Seguridad Pública) 12.6%.
- **Reserves fell with the political crisis.** International reserves dropped from ₡110.2M in July to ₡82M in September (BCR figures cited by Turcios), and an IMF stand-by of US$11.25M was approved. The Treasury did not borrow from the Central Bank.
- **The surplus was lent to the autonomous sector.** More than a third of the Treasury's resources (₡17.4M, 36%) were bonds of CEL, CEPA and the Valle de La Esperanza and BCR participation certificates.
- **Direct debt fell** for the third year, to ₡35.6M (20.9% of revenue), but **state guarantees rose ₡16.5M to ₡182.5M** — five times direct debt and 107% of revenue.

Overall: **recovery under way, near-balance, a modestly rebuilt buffer, but thin cash, falling reserves and an unchanged dependence on trade taxes** — in the year of the University assault (2 September), the state of siege (5 September) and the coup of 26 October.

Currency: US\$1.00 = ₡2.50, fixed.

### 2.3 The income tax

The income and road tax fell ₡2.66M (−13.5%), its first decline since 1955. The Memoria explains about half of it: ₡1.25M of prior-year assessments had been booked in 1959 as a one-off. Net of that, the decline was ₡1.41M (about −7.6%). A lag of income-tax collections behind the 1958–59 downturn is a plausible reading of the remainder, but the Memoria does not make it. The income tax was 10.0% of revenue — still the fourth-largest tax.

*The security pillar is the Defense-controlled apparatus: the Guardia Nacional, Policía de Hacienda and Policía Nacional were all part of the Ministry of Defense and commanded by military officers (project author’s institutional knowledge). Estado 4 places the Policía Nacional and Policía de Hacienda in Seguridad Pública; no separate Guardia Nacional unit appears in any ramo (it is most plausibly funded within Fuerza Armada). Interior contains no police units; its Dirección General de Migración (₡274,581.67) is the only security-type unit and is kept outside the pillar.*

Following the project author’s classification, Agricultura y Ganadería is excluded from the development pillar: in the agro-export economy of the 1950s the ministry mainly supported the export sector (coffee and cotton research, extension and credit) rather than broad-based development. Public works is kept in the development pillar: even where infrastructure chiefly served the export model, roads, water and electricity were public goods used by the wider population, with second-order benefits for rural inhabitants. The main electricity investment (CEL’s Río Lempa project) was financed largely outside the budget, through the autonomous agency and state guarantees, so the budget pillar understates infrastructure spending.

With revenue recovering, development spending rose 8.5% (education +11.2%, public works +8.2%) while security fell 1.6%. The 1960 budget was voted and mostly executed under Lemus; only the last two months fell under the Junta.

## 8. Assessment

**Overall.** In 1960 the fiscal position stabilized. Revenue recovered by 9%, the deficit nearly closed, the surplus rose and debt kept falling. The data show:

- **A recovery led by imports, not coffee.** Coffee export duties stayed far below their 1957 level; the revenue gain came from import duties. Dependence on trade taxes increased.
- **Spending restraint continued, but composition shifted to development.** Credits used were flat; development took 45.7% of credits used, with education at its decade high, while the security share was unchanged.
- **Fiscal space was thinner than the surplus suggests.** Treasury cash fell to ₡8.4M, part of the surplus was in doubtful receivables, and four-fifths of payments were rigid. A renewed revenue shock would have had little investment left to cut.
- **Contingent exposure kept growing.** Guarantees of autonomous-institution debt, mainly the Banco Hipotecario’s mortgage bonds and CEL’s borrowing, reached ₡182.5M. The State was shrinking its own debt while lending its credit to the autonomous sector.
- **Political risk, not fiscal risk, was realized.** In a year of near-balance the regime faced the largest urban rebellion of the decade and was overthrown. The budget gave education a record share while the Defense-controlled apparatus, on a flat budget, stormed the University. The fiscal signature of 1960 is therefore not a deficit but a composition: a development pillar producing the educated public that the security pillar was used to contain.
- **No fiscal collapse preceded the coup.** The October 1960 coup took place in a year of fiscal recovery, after two years of deficits and investment cuts. The fiscal accounts do not show a government that had run out of money; any fiscal contribution to the political crisis must be sought in the 1958–59 adjustment and its distribution, not in a 1960 exhaustion.
