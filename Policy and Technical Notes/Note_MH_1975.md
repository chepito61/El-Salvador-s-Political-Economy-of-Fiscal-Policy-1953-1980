**Political economy reading of 1975.**

- **The tax side moved.** Income tax rose by half and a new sugar export duty appeared. Direct taxes rose to 26.7% of current revenue, from about 23% in 1971–74. The regime was taxing the 1973–74 boom profits and the sugar windfall at last.
- **Coffee was treated differently.** The coffee duty fell 13% in a year of flat prices. In the same year the Treasury lent ₡30M to the coffee company. The 1975 accounts do not say why. The coincidence is worth checking (Tracks A and C).
- **The spending surge slowed but did not stop.** Credits used rose 19%, the same as revenue. The comparable deficit widened again, to 10% of revenue.
- **Security spending stalled, but repression did not.** Defence grew 1% in credits used. Yet 1975 is the year of the 30 July massacre and of the birth of the BPR. Repression was cheap in budget terms; its cost was political.
- **Reform was announced (ISTA) while the street was put down.** The pattern of Track D starts here: reform from above, repression of autonomous organization from below.

Currency: US$1.00 = ₡2.50.

- **Income tax rose by half and became the largest single tax (₡118.9M), ahead of excises and of all export duties.** Of the ₡118.9M, ₡92.6M was accrued rather than collected in cash (p. 26). The Memoria credits "the reforms introduced in tax administration".

- **A sugar export duty yielded ₡25.6M** in its first year, a third of the coffee duty. It taxed the 1974–75 sugar price boom (Track A).
- **Import duties stalled** (−0.3%), and the stamp tax grew 13% after +44% in 1974.

**Tax burden (Memoria, Cuadro 11): 11.9% of GDP** (estimated GDP ₡4,564.7M). The 1974 ratio is revised to 11.5% and 1973 to 11.3%. The elasticity to national income was 1.22 (1974: 1.09).

## 9. Preliminary Observations

1. **Income tax +49%,** now the largest tax; company income tax +47%; direct taxes 26.7% of revenue.
2. **Coffee duty −13% on a flat price; a new sugar duty of ₡25.6M.**
3. **Spending +19%,** led by agriculture (+47%) and health (+32%); Defence +1%.
4. **The comparable deficit reached 10.3% of revenue,** the widest since 1967, hidden by a ₡101.6M paper surplus built on ₡144M of unsold bonds.
5. **The Treasury lent ₡30M to the coffee company,** off budget.
6. **Direct debt +23%,** for the fourth year.
7. **The 1974 unit lines are now filled:** teachers +23%, University +44%, Fuerza Armada +36%, IRA ×3.
8. **The 30 July massacre and the birth of the BPR,** in a year when the security budget barely grew.

## 10. Reading 1975 Against the Hypotheses

| Hypothesis | 1975 evidence (and 1974 unit lines) | Verdict |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **H1** Education and the University squeezed | Education +18% in 1975, share flat at 23.4%; social development 40.2%. In 1974 the University subsidy rose 44%. The students were shot on 30 July 1975 | **No budget squeeze;** the conflict with the University is political, not fiscal |
| **H2** Wage containment, especially teachers | Remunerations +13.1% (1975); teachers +23.1% in 1974, above the average | **Not contained in money.** Real terms need a CPI. Contract pay in the teachers' line (₡1.55M in 1974) gives a first handle on the substitute-teacher question |
| **H3** Public investment cut first | Capital +25%; public works +3%; agriculture and health capital up | **Investment continued,** shifting from roads to agriculture and health, mostly loan-financed |
| **H4** Monetary policy | No Central Bank financing in the accounts; ₡100M of bonds authorized and not sold; Treasury cash fell | **No monetary financing;** the deficit was financed by loans, arrears and the earlier surplus |
| **H5** IRA food subsidies cut | IRA support tripled in 1974 (₡9.8M); AID grain-storage loan ₡12.7M in 1975 | **Expanded** |
| *Track A: tax bargain* | Income tax +49%; sugar duty new; property +20%; coffee duty −13% | **The bargain moved:** income and sugar taxed, coffee eased |
| *Track B: safety valve* | None | **Closed** |
| *Track C: protection bargain* | ₡30M Treasury loan to the coffee company; Defence flat; 30 July massacre | **Support to the coffee elite through credit,** repression through existing forces |
| *Track D: reform diluted* | ISTA created; IDB land-distribution project; the 1976 project would be withdrawn | **Reform announced;** see 1976 |

**The story in one paragraph.** In 1975 coffee prices were flat and the coffee duty fell, but revenue still rose a fifth because income tax jumped by half and a new sugar duty taxed the sugar boom. The regime kept spending: agriculture and health above all, with the IDB, the World Bank and AID financing irrigation, schools and grain storage. Defence barely grew. The comparable deficit reached 10% of revenue, covered by loans and by a paper surplus of bonds that were never sold. Off budget, the Treasury lent ₡30M to the coffee company. On the street, the Army and the security forces shot the students marching against the cost of the Miss Universe pageant, and the BPR was born a few weeks later. **The pattern for 1975 is "tax income and sugar, support coffee, spend on the countryside, and shoot the students".** The Brazilian frost of July 1975 was about to give the regime the largest windfall in its history.
