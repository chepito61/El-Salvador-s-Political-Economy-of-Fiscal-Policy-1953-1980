# El Salvador: Fiscal Year 1979 — Coffee Revenue Returns, Security Spending Jumps, and the Regime Falls

**Working Note — Evidence from the Informe Complementario Constitucional (November 2026)**

> **About this note.** This is the working note on the 1979 Memoria, in the format of the 1952–1978 series. It ends with sections on the cost of the war and on the hypotheses (H1–H5) and the four tracks of the *Political Economy Context* note. Every figure was read on the page image and checked by arithmetic. The IMF-style diagnostic is in the companion *Technical Note 1979*.
>
> **What this volume contains.** The 1979 Memoria comes in two scanned parts (MH 1979 A, 34 pages; B, 33 pages). Part A has the narrative (Cuadros 1–25) and Estado I-4 (revenue against estimates). Part B has Estado II-1 (revenue by item, 1975–1979), **Estado III-9 (public debt by instrument at 31 December 1979)** and **Estado IV-2 (spending by unit for the 1978 budget, liquidated at 31 December 1979)**. So this note gives the **1978 unit lines**. The first page of Estado II-1 (income tax, 1975–1979) is missing from the scan; the 1979 income tax lines are taken from Estado I-4.
>
> **A new government reports on the old one's year.** The volume is addressed to the *Junta Revolucionaria de Gobierno*. It is signed on 26 March 1980 by José Carvajal Mátal (Director of Central Accounting) and Roberto Alvergue Vides, Minister of Finance since 15 January 1980, who notes that the 1979 budget "was executed under the direction of my predecessors".
>
> **Corrections to the 1978 notes.** Two errors in the 1978 notes come to light here; both carry dated corrections. (1) The 1978 underlying result double-counted ₡35.8M of loan-financed on-lending, which was already inside the loan-financed commitments; corrected, it is −₡226.2M (−22.0% of revenue), not −₡190.4M (−18.5%). (2) ISTA had placed ₡17.6M of its ₡50M agrarian bonds by end-1978; the 1978 notes said they were wholly unused.

**Political economy reading of 1979.**

- **Coffee revenue came back, by volume more than price.** The coffee duty tripled (₡126.9M to ₡379.5M) while the price rose only 6.6%. Exports reached record volumes in 1979 (World Bank, 1983); part of the 1978 crop, held back in 1978, was sold in 1979. Current revenue rose 18.4% to ₡1,215.4M.
- **Security spending jumped.** Defensa +42% (capital ₡52.2M, against ₡14.8M in 1978) and Seguridad Pública +71%. Security reached **12.7% of credits used**, the highest of the 1968–1979 series. Defensa's appropriation rose from ₡81.7M to ₡129.4M in the year; only ₡2.6M of that was a legislative credit, so almost all of it came from **transfers between appropriations ordered by the executive.**
- **Education, health and agriculture fell in real terms** (−7%, −13% and −20%).
- **The tax bargain moved back.** Company income tax fell 6.0%; the Memoria attributes the lower income tax to **"incentive measures decreed in December 1978 for the taxation of enterprises"**. The agricultural land tax fell 24%.
- **The deficit narrowed but stayed large:** the comparable deficit fell from 22.0% to 9.3% of revenue. The Memoria shows a "budget surplus" of ₡98.4M only because it counts ₡200M of new bonds as revenue, half of them unsold.
- **The regime fell in the year its finances recovered.** The coup of 15 October came after the Cathedral killings, the fall of Somoza and capital flight. The budget the Junta inherited had the highest security share and the lowest development share of 1974–1979.

Currency: US$1.00 = ₡2.50.

- **The coffee duty tripled on a 6.6% price rise.** The duty is progressive in the price, but the main driver was volume: the World Bank (1983) reports record export volumes in 1979–80, about 60% above 1977–78. Ellacuría (December 1978) describes a 1978 marketing policy "based on speculation on price increases", which held coffee back.
- **"Other merchandise" export duties fell back to ₡12.5M** (₡116.6M in 1978) as the coffee duty recovered. The two lines move in opposite directions, which supports the reading that the 1978 "other merchandise" line was coffee-related. **[Still an open question: no law or product is named.]**
- **Company income tax fell 6.0%,** on 1978 profits and after the December 1978 incentives. It was 19.3% of the coffee duty (61% in 1978).
- **The tax burden was 13.2% of GDP** (Memoria, Cuadro 18; GDP estimated at ₡8,825.7M). GDP is revised again: 1976 13.5%, 1977 15.9%, 1978 12.7%.

## 9. Preliminary Observations

1. **Coffee duty ×3 on a 6.6% price rise** (volume and the release of 1978 stocks); current revenue +18.4%.
2. **Security +50%; 12.7% of credits used, the highest of the series.** Defensa's appropriation rose ₡47.7M in the year, almost all by executive transfer.
3. **Education −7%, health −13%, agriculture −20% in real terms.**
4. **Company income tax −6%,** after enterprise incentives decreed in December 1978.
5. **The comparable deficit narrowed to 9.3% of revenue** (22.0% in 1978); the Memoria's "surplus" rests on ₡200M of bonds, half unsold.
6. **Direct debt 70.6% of revenue;** open commitments ₡667M (55% of revenue).
7. **1978 unit lines:** teachers +1.5% real; University −6.5%; Fuerza Armada +9%; Seguridad Pública +21%; Defensa administration ×2.6; Presidency information ×1.9; IRA and ISTA −6%.

## 10. Reading 1979 Against the Hypotheses

| Hypothesis | 1979 evidence (and 1978 unit lines) | Verdict |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **H1** Education and the University squeezed | Education −7.4% real (1979); University subsidy −6.5% real (1978) | **Yes, in both years** |
| **H2** Wage containment, especially teachers | Teachers' line +1.5% real (1978); remunerations +1.3% real (1979); WB: real minimum wages fell sharply in 1978–79 | **Yes** |
| **H3** Public investment cut first | Real investment +36% in 1979 (works ₡149.6M), financed by bonds and loans | **No in 1979:** investment recovered with the revenue |
| **H4** Monetary policy | Central Bank notes repaid; net repayment to the banking system (WB); ₡100M of bonds unsold; capital flight (WB 1983; Ellacuría) | **Fiscal monetary financing reversed; private capital left** |
| **H5** IRA food subsidies cut | IRA −6% real (1978) | **Squeezed** |
| *Track A: tax bargain* | Company income tax −6% after December 1978 incentives; land tax −24% | **Reversed** |
| *Track B: safety valve* | Dignity bonds untouched | **Closed** |
| *Track C: protection bargain* | Security +50%; Defensa +₡47.7M by executive transfer; Defensa administration ×2.6 (1978); Cathedral killings; coup | **Security first, then collapse of the regime** |
| *Track D: reform diluted* | ISTA −6% real (1978); ISTA bonds ₡43M used; COFINTA ₡200M land bonds authorized; Junta freezes holdings above 98 ha | **Reform reappears on paper at the end of the year** |

**The story in one paragraph.** In 1979 coffee revenue came back, mostly through record export volumes: the duty tripled and current revenue rose 18%. The government used the recovery to rebuild investment and, above all, security. Defensa rose 42% and Seguridad Pública 71%; most of Defensa's extra ₡47.7M came from transfers between appropriations rather than a vote of the Assembly, and security reached an eighth of the budget, while education, health and agriculture fell in real terms. The 1978 unit lines show the same choice a year earlier: the security forces, Defence administration and the Presidency's publicity grew while the University and the food and agrarian institutes lost ground. Company income tax fell after new incentives for enterprises. The comparable deficit narrowed to 9% of revenue, but only half of the ₡200M bond issue was sold, the paper surplus rested on ₡335M of unsold bonds, and direct debt passed 70% of revenue. On 15 October young officers deposed Romero. **The pattern for 1979 is "coffee money returns, security takes the margin, the tax bargain is relaxed, and the regime falls anyway".**
