# El Salvador: Fiscal Diagnostic and Vulnerability Assessment, 1963

**Technical Note — Consolidation by Conversion: Central Bank Credit Becomes Bonds, Investment Moves to Foreign-Financed Budgets (November 2026)**

> **About this note.** Fiscal diagnostic for 1963, the first full year of the Rivera government. It follows the structure of the diagnostic series for 1952–1962. All figures come from the scanned *Informe Complementario Constitucional* for 1963 (signed 29 February 1964 by Minister Alvaro Marino); 1961–62 comparisons from the revised notes. Every figure was read on the page image and checked by arithmetic. The budget balance is reported on a commitment basis and an approximate cash basis.
>
> Pillars: development = education + health + public works; security = Defensa Nacional, which since 1962 includes the police (Cuerpos de Seguridad Pública).
>
> **Classification change.** From 1963 the budget groups spending into *Gastos de Funcionamiento* (operation + equipment), *Gastos de Capital–Propiedad*, *Transferencias* (subsidies, *subvenciones* and a new class of *aportes*), *Clases Pasivas*, *Deuda Pública* and *Egresos que no representan gastos*. Estado 5 still uses the old class columns, so the series by class remains comparable on a payments basis.

---

## Executive Summary

**The fiscal position is assessed as "moderate and rising debt, low-to-moderate stress, high vulnerability."** Stress eased: revenue grew 7%, the deficit narrowed to 3% of revenue, and the Superávit Financiero turned positive again. But the improvement came largely from converting ₡10M of Central Bank credit into long-term bonds, so the debt rose by a third.

- **Revenue grew 7.1%, now led by consumption taxes.** Rentas rose ₡11.9M to ₡180.5M. Consumption taxes rose 21% (new taxes on locally refined petroleum and on flour; higher yield on alcohol). Coffee duties rose 8.5% despite a lower world price. The income tax rose only 5.2% and fell ₡5.3M short of its estimate. Total income was 6.6% below the budget estimate.
- **Spending rose moderately.** Credits used rose 3.5% to ₡186.3M; payments 1.8%.
- **The deficit narrowed:** −₡5.7M (−3.2% of revenue) on a commitment basis; about −₡1.5M (−0.9%) on a cash basis.
- **The Central Bank advance became public debt.** By D.L. 303 (2 May 1963) the Treasury converted ₡10.0M of its 1962 short-term debt with the BCR into 6% Treasury bonds. The conversion was counted as a resource of the year, which turned the 1962 Déficit Financiero (−₡6.9M) into a small surplus (+₡0.48M). ₡6.0M of BCR notes remained outstanding.
- **Investment moved out of the ordinary budget.** Ordinary capital credits fell another 27% (₡10.3M → ₡7.5M), while foreign-financed extraordinary budgets spent about ₡12.2M and committed ₡14.5M more — led by the new IDA-financed **Programa Nacional de Carreteras** and the Eximbank/AID airport.
- **Composition:** development rose to 47.1% of credits used (education 22.1%, public works recovering to 13.7%); security 12.2%; the presidency fell 39%.
- **Debt ₡48.8M** (27.0% of revenue), up ₡12.5M; guarantees ₡209.5M (116% of revenue), with the BCR's IDB borrowing up ₡10.5M.

Currency: US$1.00 = ₡2.50, fixed.

**How the surplus reappeared.** The budget itself was still in deficit (−₡5.1M including otros ingresos). The Memoria then adds the ₡10.0M conversion of the BCR advance and ₡1.57M of cancelled reservations as resources, which more than offset the opening deficit of ₡6.9M (MH 1963, pp. 35–36, 48–49). A reform of the Ley Orgánica de Presupuestos (D.L. 290, April 1963) allowed cancelled reservations to be counted as resources of the year. The return to surplus is therefore a refinancing result, not a fiscal one.

**The coffee channel.** Coffee duties rose 8.5% (₡19.93M → ₡21.63M) although the Pink Sheet price fell 3.2% (US$0.83 → US$0.81/kg): volume and timing, not price, drove the duty. Import duties were flat (+0.4%) despite the growth of the economy, consistent with the spread of Central American free trade and industrial exemptions.

## 8. Assessment

**Overall: moderate and rising debt, low-to-moderate stress, high vulnerability.** Compared with 1962 ("low but rising debt, high but easing stress"), the flow position improved markedly, but through consolidation of Central Bank credit into long-term debt rather than a structural surplus.

- **Revenue diversified further.** Consumption taxes, including new taxes on refined petroleum and flour, carried the growth; trade taxes fell to 45.9% of revenue. The income tax stalled after its 1962 jump and missed its estimate by 16%.
- **The deficit closed on paper.** The ₡10M conversion of BCR credit into bonds was counted as a resource, turning the financial position positive. Treasury cash fell to ₡4.8M.
- **Investment was restructured.** The ordinary budget's own capital spending fell to 4% of credits used, while foreign-financed programmes (IDA roads, Eximbank/AID airport, US school grants) spent 1.6 times as much and committed more. The ordinary budget's share in investment now takes the form of counterpart *aportes* and debt service — the mechanism the project author identified for Central America.
- **The development pillar grew, but its content changed.** Public works rose as transfers to a loan-financed programme, not as direct construction.
