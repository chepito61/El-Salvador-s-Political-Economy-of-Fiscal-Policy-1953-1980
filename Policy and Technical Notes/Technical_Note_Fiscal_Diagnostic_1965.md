# El Salvador: Fiscal Diagnostic and Vulnerability Assessment, 1965

**Technical Note — Spending at the Peak: A Loan-Financed Capital Budget and the Return of the Deficit (November 2026)**

> **About this note.** Fiscal diagnostic for 1965, the fourth year of the Rivera government. It follows the structure of the diagnostic series for 1952–1964. All figures come from the scanned *Informe Complementario Constitucional* for 1965 (signed 28 February 1966 by Minister Alvaro Marino); 1963–64 comparisons from the revised notes. Every figure was read on the page image and checked by arithmetic.
>
> Pillars: development = education + health + public works; security = Ramo de Defensa, including the police (Seguridad Pública).
>
> **Basis.** 1965 uses the same budget structure as 1964 (operating and capital budgets). Commitments by ramo are not published, so the composition of spending is on **payments (gastos)**.

---

## Executive Summary

**The fiscal position is assessed as "moderate and rising debt, low-to-moderate stress, high and rising vulnerability."** Stress stayed contained: the Treasury held ₡11M in cash, owed nothing to the Central Bank and raised its financial position. The vulnerability rose. Spending commitments, counterpart obligations and debt service expanded at the peak of the coffee cycle, a year before the price turned down.

- **Revenue +6.7% to ₡224.5M**, led by consumption taxes (+₡9.3M) and the coffee duty (+₡5.6M, on volume; the price was flat). The income tax fell 3.1%: the company tax dropped 19%.
- **Spending +15.9%** (gastos ₡222.9M), with capital spending up 70% (₡45.0M). Credits used ₡239.0M.
- **Deficit returned:** −₡14.4M on a commitment basis (−6.4% of revenue). It was financed by ₡18.2M of foreign loans; excluding loan-financed spending, the budget was in surplus (+₡3.8M).
- **Operating saving ₡41.5M** financed two-thirds of a ₡55.9M capital budget (23% of credits used).
- **Superávit Financiero ₡17.5M** (+₡3.6M), after spending ₡7.9M of the 1964 surplus through an additional credit.
- **Composition (gastos):** development 44.9% (48.8% excluding AID-financed spending), security 10.9%, education 22.0%. The University's transfer rose 52%.
- **Direct debt ₡74.9M** (33.4% of revenue, +27%); guarantees ₡130.2M (58% of revenue, +14%).

Currency: US$1.00 = ₡2.50, fixed.

## 8. Assessment

**Overall: moderate and rising debt, low-to-moderate stress, high and rising vulnerability.** Compared with 1964 ("low stress"), the government turned the windfall into a permanent expansion: wages, transfers and a capital budget co-financed by foreign loans.

- **Revenue growth slowed and its quality fell.** Coffee volume and petroleum consumption carried it; the income tax receded.
- **Spending grew 2.4 times as fast as revenue.** The deficit returned, financed by foreign loans rather than the Central Bank.
- **The loan mechanism is now central.** A third of the capital budget was loan-financed, and loan-linked obligations reached 11% of revenue.
- **Security fell as a share and development rose.** The University's transfer jumped 52%, while primary teachers' salaries lagged the payroll.
- **Vulnerability moved forward in time.** Debt, guarantees and recurrent commitments were set at peak revenue; the coffee price fell in 1966.
