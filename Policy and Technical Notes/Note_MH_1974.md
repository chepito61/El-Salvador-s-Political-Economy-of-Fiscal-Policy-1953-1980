# El Salvador: Fiscal Year 1974 — Oil Shock, a Spending Surge, and the La Cayetana Massacre

**Working Note — Evidence from the Informe Complementario Constitucional (November 2026)**

> **About this note.** This is the working note on the 1974 Memoria, in the format of the 1952–1973 series. It ends with sections on the cost of the war and on the hypotheses (H1–H5) and the four tracks of the *Political Economy Context* note. **President:** Colonel Arturo Armando Molina (PCN).

- **The Informe** is dated 20 March 1975.

| 1974 | ₡54M of bonds authorized for the investment plan; **none needed to be sold** | MH 1974, p. 5 |

**Political economy reading of 1974.**

- **A high-revenue year that turned into a spending surge.** Revenue rose 20%. Spending commitments rose 32%: investment +66%, Defence +37%, the Presidency +52%, agriculture +68%. **The comparable deficit rose to about 9% of revenue,** the worst since 1966, in a year of record coffee revenue.
- **Wages were raised in the face of inflation.** Remunerations rose 18.6% as the oil shock hit prices. It is the first time a boom-year windfall went substantially into pay. It was a defensive response to inflation, not a real gain.
- **Repression moved to the countryside.** La Cayetana is one of the first documented massacres of organized peasants in the 1970s. Its pattern recurs over the following years: organized peasants, a Guard raid, an official story of an "ambush", and the Assembly refusing to investigate. **The security budget grew 30% that year.**
- **The fiscal mechanism changes in 1974.** Until 1973 the regime spent its windfalls on works and debt repayment and contained wages. In 1974 it expanded everything at once: wages, works, security and the Presidency. This is the beginning of the large public sector of the late 1970s.

Currency: US$1.00 = ₡2.50.

**Three findings stand out:**

- **Company income tax rose 57%,** to ₡32.3M: the 1973 coffee-boom profits taxed with a one-year lag. It has nearly tripled since 1970 (₡11.6M). **For the first time it is a third of the coffee duty.**

**Tax burden (Memoria): 11.4% of GDP** (preliminary). The elasticity to national income was 1.02, as the nominal income boom carried the tax base. The Memoria (p. 24) still calls the system "inelastic". It expects the burden to rise from 1975 with the new taxes approved in the second half of 1974.

## 9. Preliminary Observations

1. **Revenue +20%:** the coffee duty reached ₡90M and company income tax ₡32M; the stamp tax rose 44%.
2. **Spending +32%:** investment +66%, Defence +37%, the Presidency +52%, agriculture +68%.
3. **Remunerations +18.6%,** a defensive wage policy against the oil-shock inflation.
4. **The comparable deficit rose to 8.9% of revenue,** hidden by a rising financial surplus and unsold bonds.
5. **Direct debt +25%** for the third year; external service +63%.
6. **Social development fell to 39% of spending,** the lowest in the series.
7. **The La Cayetana massacre** marked the move of repression to the countryside, in a year when the security budget grew 30%.
8. **Two-thirds of the war bonds are held by the Treasury itself.**

## 10. Reading 1974 Against the Hypotheses

| Hypothesis | 1974 evidence | Verdict |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **H1** Education and the University squeezed | Education +23% in credits used, but its share fell from 25.4% to 23.6%; social development fell to 39%; University line not available | **Relative squeeze:** education grew more slowly than the budget |
| **H2** Wage containment, especially teachers | Remunerations +18.6% under the wage policy; teachers' line not available | **Not contained in money,** but probably eroded in real terms by inflation. Needs the unit data and a CPI |
| **H3** Public investment cut first | Capital +66%, commitments +76% | **Investment surged** on the boom and on external loans |
| **H4** Monetary policy | Central Bank notes ₡1.2M; bonds authorized but not sold | **No monetary financing;** the surplus and commitments financed the deficit |
| **H5** IRA food subsidies cut | AID grain-marketing loan for the IRA (₡2.4M); subsidy line not available | **Expanded through a loan** as grain prices rose |
| *Track A: tax bargain* | Company income tax +57%; coffee duty ₡90M; new taxes in late 1974; stamp tax +44% | **More taxation of the boom, at the border and on transactions;** property taxes fell |
| *Track B: safety valve* | None | **Closed** |
| *Track C: protection bargain* | Defensa +37%; Presidency +52%; La Cayetana | **Security spending and repression rise together** |
| *Track D: reform diluted* | Agricultural spending +68% (irrigation, grain marketing); no land reform yet | **Development spending in the countryside instead of land redistribution** (the Agrarian Transformation comes in 1975–76) |

**The story in one paragraph.** In 1974 coffee stayed high, oil prices tripled and the regime spent. Revenue rose a fifth, but credits used rose a third. Investment, defence, the Presidency and agriculture grew fastest, and the Government raised pay by nearly a fifth to keep up with inflation. The comparable deficit quadrupled, to about 9% of revenue, but the accounts showed a rising surplus because the 1973 windfall and unsold bonds carried it. In the countryside, where the irrigation and grain programmes were being built, the National Guard massacred peasants at La Cayetana and the Assembly refused to investigate. **The pattern for 1974 is "spend the boom on everything, including security, and repress the peasants".** It marks the end of the regime's fiscal caution, two years before the coffee price explosion of 1976–77.
