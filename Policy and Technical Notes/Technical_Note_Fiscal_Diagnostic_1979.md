# El Salvador: Fiscal Diagnostic and Vulnerability Assessment, 1979

**Technical Note — Revenue Recovers, Security Takes the Margin, the Regime Falls (November 2026)**

> **About this note.** This is the fiscal diagnostic for 1979. It follows the structure of the diagnostic series for 1952–1978.
>
> **Sources.** The *Informe Complementario Constitucional* 1979, in two scanned parts:
> - part A: the analytical narrative (Cuadros 1–25) and Estado I-4 (revenue against estimates);
> - part B: Estado II-1 (revenue, 1975–1979), Estado III-9 (public debt by instrument, end-1979) and Estado IV-2 (spending by unit, **1978 budget liquidated at end-1979**).
>
> Ministry comparisons are on credits used (Cuadro 10). Every figure was read on the page image and checked by arithmetic. The volume is addressed to the Revolutionary Government Junta and signed on 26 March 1980.
>
> **Correction carried forward.** The 1978 underlying result is corrected to −₡226.2M (−22.0% of revenue); the first issue double-counted ₡35.8M of loan-financed on-lending. See the dated correction in the 1978 notes.
>
> **Pillars.** Development = education + health + public works. Security = Defensa + Ramo de Seguridad Pública.

---

## Executive Summary

**The fiscal position is assessed as "revenue recovered by coffee volume; deficit halved but still large; security spending surging, social spending falling in real terms; tax concessions to enterprises; paper surplus built on unsold bonds; direct debt far above threshold; regime collapse".**

- **Revenue.** Current revenue +18.4% to ₡1,215.4M.
  - Coffee duty ₡379.5M (+199%), **31.2% of revenue**, on a 6.6% price rise.
  - Income tax ₡192.9M (−4.4%); company income tax ₡73.2M (−6.0%), after enterprise incentives decreed in December 1978.
  - Tax / GDP 13.2% (Memoria).
- **Spending.** Credits used +8.9% to ₡1,453.3M; gastos +6.8%; commitments +18.3%.
  - **Defensa +42.4%; Seguridad Pública +71.1%;** education +5.6%; health −0.8%; agriculture −8.2% (CPI +14.1%).
  - Defensa's appropriation rose ₡47.7M during the year, ₡2.6M of it by legislative credit and the rest by executive transfers.
  - Remunerations +15.6% (about +1.3% real); real investment +35.8%.
- **Balance.** Commitment basis −₡238.0M (−19.6%). **Underlying −₡112.6M (−9.3%),** after −22.0% in 1978.
- **Liquidity.** Treasury cash ₡90.9M; Central Bank notes repaid; Superávit Financiero ₡97.0M, of which ₡100M is the unsold half of the 1979 bond issue (₡335M of unsold bonds in all; about −₡238M without them).
- **Debt.** Direct debt +34.3% to ₡857.7M (**70.6% of revenue**); guaranteed ₡688.2M (+13.6%); national public debt ₡1,603.0M (+22.7%).
- **Composition (credits used):** development 45.3%; **security 12.7%** (9.2% in 1978).

Currency: US$1.00 = ₡2.50.

## 9. Assessment

**Overall: revenue recovered by coffee volume; deficit halved but still large; security spending surging, social spending falling in real terms; tax concessions to enterprises; paper surplus built on unsold bonds; direct debt far above threshold; regime collapse.**

- **The revenue recovery was a coffee event.** The duty tripled on record volumes; concentration returned to the boom level.
- **The margin went to security.** Security spending rose 50%, to an eighth of the budget, and Defensa's appropriation was raised mostly by executive transfer; development fell 9% in real terms.
- **The tax bargain was relaxed at the wrong moment.** Enterprise incentives cut company income tax just as the cost of security and debt rose.
- **Financing became harder.** Half the bond issue was unsold, capital left the country, and direct debt passed 70% of revenue; the paper surplus is again an accounting result.
- **The fiscal choices of 1977–79 matched the political ones.** Security first, the University and the agrarian institute squeezed, the tax base untouched: the government that made those choices was overthrown on 15 October 1979.
