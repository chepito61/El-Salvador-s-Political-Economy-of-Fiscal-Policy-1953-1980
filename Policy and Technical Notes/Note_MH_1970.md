# El Salvador: Fiscal Year 1970 — The Coffee Windfall Pays for the War, the Police Get a Ministry, the State Absorbs the IRA's Losses

**Working Note — Evidence from the Informe Complementario Constitucional (November 2026)**

> **About this note.** This is the working note on the 1970 Memoria, in the format of the 1952–1969 series. It ends with sections on the cost of the war (continuing the series begun in 1969) and on the hypotheses (H1–H5) and the four tracks of the *Political Economy Context* note. **Political economy reading of 1970.**

- **The coffee windfall.** The coffee price rose 31%, revenue 16%, and the coffee duty 63%. The windfall allowed the regime to:
  - erase the 1969 war deficit;
  - repay half of the Central Bank notes;
  - settle most of its social-security arrears, in land;
  - absorb the IRA's accumulated losses;
  - raise investment by half.

  **This is the coffee cycle working through the budget, as the project's hypothesis predicts, but on the upside.** In a boom the State paid its debts and expanded. The question is whether it made lasting commitments it could not keep when coffee fell again (it did in 1971).
- **Political closure (Almeida's "threat" phase).** The PCN's 1970 landslide reversed the opposition's gains of 1964–68. The agrarian reform congress produced no law or budget line. The first guerrilla organization (FPL) was founded the same year. **The fiscal windfall bought no political opening.**
- **The police got their own ministry.** The *Ramo de Seguridad Pública* (₡10.7M) was separated from Defensa (₡15.5M). The two together were slightly below 1969's ordinary Defensa (₡27.6M), but the internal-security apparatus now had a minister and a budget of its own. This is relevant to Stanley's thesis (track C).
- **Food subsidies became a quasi-fiscal problem.** The IRA's losses on stabilizing grain prices had been financed by the Central Bank. The Treasury took them over with bonds. H5 needs re-reading: food subsidies were not only cut in the budget, they were pushed into Central Bank credit until the bill was too large to ignore.

Currency: US$1.00 = ₡2.50.

**Three findings stand out:**

- **Coffee did most of the work.** The export duties rose ₡21.07M, more than half of the ₡38.90M revenue increase. The price rose 31%. The duty rose twice as fast because of the progressive scale of 1950.
- **Customs recovered to 40.3% of revenue,** but 4.8 points of it was the temporary CACM stabilization tax. Without it customs would have been 35.5%.
- **A new tax measure.** The stamp-tax reform of September 1970 raised ₡3.69M in four months.

- New external loans contracted in 1970: AID educational television (₡4.75M); BCIE Santa Ana–Metapán–Anguiatú road (₡10.0M).

### 7.3 What the war cost the 1970 budget

The direct cost was small in 1970: ₡0.45M of emergency spending and about ₡1.85M of interest. **The war's main legacy in 1970 was financial:**

- **Repaying the Central Bank.** ₡10M was repaid out of the coffee windfall.
- **Absorbing the 1969 deficit.** ₡9.9M of the 1970 operating saving went to it.
- **A war-bond debt of ₡32.8M,** now two-thirds of domestic debt.

**Without the coffee boom, the 1970 budget would have had to choose between servicing the war debt and continuing to invest.** The windfall postponed that choice.

**Not in the budget:**

- **the returnees from Honduras.** No line again in 1970;
- **the loss of the Honduran market.** Honduras virtually withdrew from the Common Market in early 1971;
- **the January 1970 agrarian congress,** which raised land reform as the answer to the returnees and the landless, and which led to no law or budget line.

## 9. Preliminary Observations

1. **The coffee boom.** The price rose 31%, the coffee duty 63% and revenue 16%. It was the strongest revenue year since the early 1960s.
2. **The windfall was used to clean up the war and old arrears:**
   - it erased the 1969 deficit;
   - it repaid ₡10M of Central Bank notes;
   - the ISSS arrears were paid in land;
   - the IRA's past losses were taken over, with bonds.
3. **Investment rose by two-thirds in credits used,** the first sustained recovery since 1965.
4. **Ordinary security fell 5%, but the police got their own ministry.**
5. **Teachers' pay grew only 3.6%** in the best fiscal year of the decade. The University's subsidy grew 15%, and the Presidency's discretionary lines grew 24%.
6. **The income tax captured nothing of the boom.** Personal income tax fell and company income tax rose only 5%. Coffee paid through the export duty alone.
7. **Unpaid orders doubled to ₡14M,** even in a boom.

## 10. Reading 1970 Against the Hypotheses

| Hypothesis | 1970 evidence | Verdict |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **H1** Education and the University squeezed | Education +9.4%; University +15%; share stable at 26% | **Not supported** in the boom |
| **H2** Wage containment, especially teachers | Payroll +6.0%, but primary teachers' pay +3.6%, the lowest of the major lines, in the best revenue year of the decade | **Supported for teachers:** the windfall did not reach them. This is the immediate background of the 53-day ANDES strike of June–July 1971 |
| **H3** Public investment cut first | Investment +67% (credits used) as coffee revenue rose | **Supported in reverse:** investment is the budget's swing variable, up in booms and down in busts |
| **H4** Monetary policy | Central Bank notes halved (₡20M → ₡10M); IRA's Central Bank debts taken over by the Treasury | **The boom allowed partial repayment of monetary financing.** The IRA shows quasi-fiscal financing of food subsidies at the Central Bank |
| **H5** IRA food subsidies cut | Subsidy ₡1.40M (+180%) plus ₡7.30M of bonds for past losses | **Reversed in 1970,** but the earlier cuts had shifted the losses to the Central Bank. The "cut" of 1966–69 was partly a hidden transfer |
| *Track A: tax bargain* | Coffee paid through the export duty (+63%); companies' income tax +5%; personal income tax −9%; a new stamp tax | **The windfall was taxed at the border only.** The income tax did not move |
| *Track B: safety valve* | Agrarian congress; no budget line for returnees; Honduras withdraws from the CACM (early 1971) | **The closure is now complete and unanswered** |
| *Track C: protection bargain* | A separate Ministry of Public Security; Presidency discretionary lines +24% | **Institutionalization of internal security** |
| *Track D: reform diluted* | Agrarian reform congress (January 1970) with no legal or fiscal follow-up | **Reform blocked before it reached the budget** |

**The story in one paragraph.** In 1970 the coffee cycle turned up, and the budget showed what the regime did with a windfall. It paid off the war's Central Bank advances. It settled its old debts to social security, with land rather than cash. It absorbed the food agency's losses, which it had been leaving with the Central Bank. It raised investment by two-thirds. It did not raise teachers' pay meaningfully or tax the boom beyond the coffee duty. At the same time politics closed: the PCN swept the elections, the agrarian congress led nowhere, the police got a ministry, and the first guerrilla group was founded. **The pattern for 1970 is "use the windfall to clean the books and build, not to buy consent".** That shapes 1971, when coffee fell again and the teachers struck for 53 days.
