# El Salvador: Fiscal Diagnostic and Vulnerability Assessment, 1972

**Technical Note — Coffee Recovers, the Deficit Narrows, Debt Keeps Rising, and the Treasury Calls Its Own Tax System Inelastic (November 2026)**

> **About this note.** This is the fiscal diagnostic for 1972. It follows the structure of the diagnostic series for 1952–1971.
>
> **Sources.** The *Informe Complementario Constitucional* 1972:
> - the new analytical narrative, with Cuadros 1–24 (tax burden, elasticity, sectoral and economic classification, current and capital accounts, debt indicators);
> - Estado II-7 (gastos by unit and class);
> - debt statements VII-1 and VII-2.
>
> There is no Estado II-5 or II-8. Revenue by item is from Cuadro 10. Ministry comparisons are on the gastos basis (II-7). Every figure was read on the page image and checked by arithmetic.
>
> **Pillars.** Development = education + health + public works. Security = Defensa + Ramo de Seguridad Pública.

---

## Executive Summary

**The fiscal position is assessed as "rising debt, easing deficit, moderate-to-high vulnerability (cyclical and political)".**

- **Revenue.** Current revenue +8.9% to ₡326.4M, 3.6% above estimate.
  - Coffee price +11.9%; export duties +24.2% (elasticity about 2).
  - Income tax +14.9% for a second year.
  - Tax / GDP 10.7% (the Memoria's first official series). The Treasury calls the system "inelastic" and blames exemptions, low property valuations and evasion.
- **Spending.** Gastos +14.2% to ₡336.3M; +7.2% excluding two book-keeping operations (ANTEL assets ₡8.85M; IDA loan on-lent to CEL ₡11.59M). Credits used +7.8% to ₡383.7M.
  - Teachers' pay +25.8%, after the 1971 strike.
  - University of El Salvador current funding −12.3%, in the year it was occupied.
  - Defensa +16.7%.
- **Deficit.** Commitment basis −₡57.3M (−17.6%). **Underlying −₡15.4M (−4.7%),** down from −6.5% in 1971. The Memoria reports a ₡13.5M surplus.
- **Liquidity.** Superávit Financiero ₡11.8M, resting on ₡86M of undrawn loans and unsold bonds. Central Bank notes ₡17.4M (from ₡10.0M). Unpaid orders ₡38.9M.
- **Debt.** Direct debt +21% to ₡205.7M (63% of revenue); service 5.7% of revenue. Total public debt ₡403.4M (14.2% of GDP).
- **Composition (gastos):** development 47.5%; security 10.0%. Excluding ANTEL and CEL: 50.5% and 10.6%.

Currency: US$1.00 = ₡2.50.

### 2.2 Tax expenditures and the Ministry's own diagnosis

The 1972 Memoria (pp. 40–41) gives the first official diagnosis of the tax system:

- **Low burden:** "one of the lowest among developing countries"; it rose less than one point in 1968–72.
- **Inelastic:** revenue grows only through new taxes or rates. Elasticities above 1 after 1969 reflect the emergency tax, the stabilization tax and the stamp-tax reform.
- **Causes:** "tax exemptions, low property valuations, specific-rate taxes" and evasion beyond the reach of current administrative and legal means.
- **Remedy proposed:** the NIT taxpayer registry (D.L. 79, August 1972) and better administration. **No reform of rates or bases is proposed.**

## 9. Assessment

**Overall: rising debt, easing deficit, moderate-to-high vulnerability (cyclical and political).**

- **The coffee recovery narrowed the deficit.** Export duties rose 24% and the income tax 15%; the underlying deficit fell to 4.7% of revenue.
- **Debt kept rising regardless.** Direct debt passed 60% of revenue. Service doubled to 5.7% of revenue. Central Bank credit rose by three-quarters. The Memoria's surplus rests on bonds not yet sold and loans not yet drawn.
- **The Treasury diagnosed its own tax system** as low-yielding and inelastic, held down by exemptions, low property valuations and evasion. It proposed administrative remedies only.
- **The budget recorded a political turn.** Teachers were paid (+26%) after the 1971 strike, but the University was occupied and lost 12% of its funding. Defensa grew faster than any large civilian ministry, and new opaque top-level lines appeared at Defence and the Presidency.
