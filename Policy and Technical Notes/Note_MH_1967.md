**Working Note — Evidence from the Informe Complementario Constitucional (November 2026)**

## Introduction: what the 1967 volumes contain

The 1967 Memoria is scanned in two volumes (72 + 72 pages):

- the narrative (printed pp. 17–43);
- Estados II-4 and II-5 (1967 revenue);
- **Estado IV-2:** the 1966 budget updated to 31 December 1967, by unit and class;
- **Estado IV-3:** payments made in 1967 against 1966 appropriations;
- Estados VII-1 and VII-2 (debt).

**The 1967 budget's own table by unit (Estado II-7) is not in the scans.** The 1968 Memoria supplies it in its Estado IV-2: the 1967 budget updated to 31 December 1968.

**This note therefore uses two bases:**

- **For totals and classes:** the 1967 Memoria (payments in 1967, ₡226.43M).
- **For ministries:** the *final execution* of each budget, meaning payments in the year plus payments the following year on that year's commitments:
  - 1966 final: ₡244.68M, from the 1967 Memoria;
  - 1967 final: ₡233.67M, from the 1968 Memoria.

The final basis is close to credits used net of cancelled commitments. It is consistent across the two years.

Currency: US$1.00 = ₡2.50.

**Customs fell to 39.6% of current revenue,** against 45.5% in 1964 (Memoria, p. 24). The coffee duty fell with the price (−7.4% against −7.3%). The Memoria attributes the gain in other taxes mainly to "measures to mobilize resources that, for lack of legal basis, had not been brought into the General Fund at the right time" (p. 26).

**Correction (from the 1969 Memoria).** The 1969 Memoria's revenue history (p. 30) states that 1967 current revenue "includes ₡4.50M of income tax withholdings transferred to the Fund". This one-off transfer is the main "mobilized resource". It explains most of the income tax gain (+₡3.95M), while property and succession taxes added ₡2.29M. **Excluding the transfer, current revenue would have been about ₡222.4M, −0.5% on 1966:** a second year of falling underlying revenue, not a small recovery.

### 1.3 Tax expenditures: what the 1967 Memoria shows

The 1966 and 1967 Memorias are the first to name tax exemptions as a cause of revenue weakness: "the regional economic integration process and especially the granting of fiscal incentives to a larger number of firms under the Ley de Fomento … explain the declining trend observed in this source of revenue" (p. 24).

| Customs (import + export) / current revenue | 45.4% | 44.3% | 43.1% | 39.6% |
| Company income tax (₡M) | 14.21 | 11.49 | 9.59 | 11.26 |
| Company income tax vs. its estimate | — | — | −28% | [estimate not yet extracted] |

*Source: MH 1964–1967, Estado II-5; MH 1967, p. 24.*

**What the Memorias do not provide** is the cost itself: duties waived on exempt imports, or income tax forgone under the incentive law. Three ways to estimate it:

1. **The effective tariff:** import duties divided by imports (c.i.f.), from BCR trade data. A falling effective rate while imports grow measures the combined erosion from regional free trade and exemptions.
2. **Composition:** duties on machinery, materials and industrial inputs, the categories exempted under the Ley de Fomento Industrial, against duties on consumer goods. In 1967 duties on machinery and manufactures fell while duties on food rose.
3. **Firm-level grants:** the executive agreements (*acuerdos*) granting benefits to individual firms, published in the Diario Oficial. These identify the beneficiaries, which matters for the political-economy question of who gained.

## 8. Preliminary Observations

1. **Second downturn year:** coffee −7%. Revenue rose 1.5% only because of a one-off transfer of ₡4.50M of income tax withholdings (MH 1969, p. 30); underlying revenue fell about 0.5%. Customs fell to under 40% of revenue.
2. **The Memoria explicitly links customs erosion to integration and fiscal incentives.** Tax expenditures are now an acknowledged policy cost.
3. **Spending was cut 2.4% (gastos) and 4.5% (final):** capital −17%, construction −40%, Agriculture −28%. Payroll still rose 3.7%.
4. **School construction collapsed (−78%),** while primary salaries (+8.6%) and the University (+20%) were protected.
5. **The IRA's subsidy was cut 23%,** the first clear cut in the series.
6. **The Treasury ran arrears and returned to Central Bank notes (₡6M)** after three years without them.
7. **Security rose faster than development in money:** the army line +14%, in the year of the presidential succession.

## 9. Reading 1967 Against the Hypotheses

| Hypothesis | 1967 evidence | Verdict |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **H1** Education and the University squeezed | Education +1.4% overall; **school construction −78%**; University +20%; primary +8.6% | **Partly supported:** the squeeze fell on education *investment*, not on the University or salaries |
| **H2** Wage containment, especially teachers | Payroll +3.7% (vs. +10.2% in 1966); primary salaries +8.6% | **Not supported for teachers;** overall wage growth slowed |
| **H3** Public investment cut first | Capital −17% (gastos), −25% (credits used); construction −40%; Agriculture −28% | **Supported, for the second year** |
| **H4** Monetary policy | Treasury returned to Central Bank notes (₡6M); arrears doubled | **Accommodation, not tightening:** the Central Bank financed the Treasury again |
| **H5** IRA food subsidies cut | IRA −23% | **Supported for the first time** |

**The story in one paragraph.** In the second downturn year the government had no buffer left. It cut what could be postponed: school buildings, agricultural programmes and public works. It also cut the IRA's food subsidy. It protected salaries and the University, and gave the army more. It closed the gap with arrears, loans from its own funds and Central Bank notes. Meanwhile:

- the presidential election excluded the left;
- ANDES was mobilizing;
- Honduras began enforcing its land reform against Salvadoran settlers.

**The pattern so far is "protect people, cut buildings and food".** It raises a new question: whether the teachers' grievances of 1967–68 came from pay levels and the hiring bottleneck rather than from cuts to the payroll.
