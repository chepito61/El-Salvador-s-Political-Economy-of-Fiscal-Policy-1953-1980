# El Salvador: Fiscal Year 1961 — From the Junta to the Directorio: Spending Through a Revenue Fall

**Working Note — Evidence from the Informe Complementario Constitucional (November 2026)**

> **About this note.** Working note on the 1961 Memoria, in the format of the 1952–1960 series, with a final section that reads the year against the project's hypotheses (H1–H5). Every figure was read on the page image and checked by arithmetic. Political periods follow Roberto Turcios: the Junta (October 1960–January 1961) as the opening toward **democratic transition**, the Directorio Cívico Militar as the start of **authoritarian reformism** (*Rebelión*, pp. 325–326). The IMF-style diagnostic is in the companion *Technical Note 1961*; verified data in "Spending Data/ministry_class_1961_1979.csv" and "pillars_1961_1979.csv".
>
> Pillars: development = education + health + public works; security = Defensa + Seguridad Pública (the Defense-controlled apparatus); agriculture, Interior and the presidency reported separately.

## Political Context

The fiscal year spans two governments. On 26 October 1960 a military–civilian Junta de Gobierno overthrew President José María Lemus. On 23 December 1960 it voted the 1961 budget (Decreto N° 52, Diario Oficial N° 240, Tomo 189). On 25 January 1961 a counter-coup replaced it with the Directorio Cívico Militar, which governed until 25 January 1962 and executed the budget almost entirely. The Minister of Finance, Humberto Bernal h., signed the Informe on 28 February 1962; the statements were signed by Mariano Novoa Flores, Director de Contabilidad Central.

## Introduction

The scanned 1961 volume has 66 pages: the narrative (printed pp. 9–30, Sections I–VII), Estado N° 1 (budget liquidation), Estados N° 2 and 3 (revenue estimated vs. collected; 1961 vs. 1960), Estado N° 5 (spending by organizational unit and class, in full, with a summary by ramo), and Estados N° 19–21 (public debt, debt service and state guarantees). **Estado N° 4 (credits used by unit, with the gastos/compromisos split) and Estados N° 6–18 and 22–28 are not reproduced**; their totals are taken from the narrative. The Memoria uses new ministry names: Ramo de Educación (formerly Cultura) and Ramo del Interior (formerly Gobernación).

| Concept | Meaning |
|:--|:--|
| *Rentas percibidas y devengadas* | Revenue of the year (collected and accrued) |
| *Gastos* | Payments made against the year's budget |
| *Compromisos pendientes de liquidación* | Credit reservations against the year's budget not yet paid at 31 December |
| *Créditos utilizados* | Gastos + compromisos: the Ministry's measure of budget execution |

## 1. Revenue Structure and Performance

### 1.1 The revenue fall

Rentas were **₡158,085,139.62**, down ₡12,461,366.39 (**−7.3%**) from ₡170,546,506.01 in 1960. With *otros ingresos* (₡3,039,148.61), total income was ₡161,124,288.23, ₡9.14M (5.4%) below the budget estimate. Revenue was 16.6% below the 1957 peak.

## 6. Extraordinary Budgets and Loan-Financed Investment

- **Carretera del Litoral** (D.L. 1702, 1954; IBRD). Spending in 1961 ₡12.69M (₡1.77M operating, ₡10.92M capital), up from ₡7.46M in 1960. General Fund contribution for 1961: ₡3.90M (as restated in MH 1962). IBRD access-roads disbursements: ₡3.97M. Debt service on the Litoral loans, paid from the ordinary budget: ₡4.30M. **Counterpart plus debt service: ₡8.2M, 5.2% of revenue** — about half of ordinary-budget capital spending. Cumulative 1955–61: ₡80.83M spent.
- **Aeropuerto Internacional de Ilopango** (D. 487 of the Directorio, 22 Dec 1961, DO 236). Financed by an Eximbank loan of US$2.65M and a Development Loan Fund loan of US$1.6M (₡10.63M), plus ₡0.21M from the General Fund. Not yet in execution at end-1961.
- **Obras del Valle de La Esperanza** and **Carretera Panamericana**: liquidated in 1961; ₡585,338.49 of balances returned to the General Fund.
- **Proveeduría General de la República**: sales ₡803,066.58 and a General Fund subsidy of ₡220,000.00; credits used ₡422,525.88.

## 8. Preliminary Observations

1. **The revenue shock came through imports.** Import duties fell 17.1% and coffee duties 12.6%, more than the 3% fall in the world coffee price. Political uncertainty after two coups, crop timing and new industrial exemptions are possible causes; the BCR data will tell.
2. **Spending rose into the downturn.** Credits used rose 4.7% and payments 7.6%, driven by education (+18.6%). The Directorio executed the Junta's budget without cuts.
3. **The payroll became heavier.** Personnel reached 56.9% of revenue. Education wages rose ₡4.6M (+19.9%).
4. **Investment paid.** Capital credits used fell about 15% and public works 4%, while the IBRD-financed Litoral highway accelerated outside the ordinary budget and its counterpart and debt service took 5.2% of revenue.
5. **The surplus was exhausted.** The Superávit Financiero fell from ₡20.3M to ₡0.5M, and the Treasury resorted to asset sales, inter-fund loans and payment arrears. Direct debt barely moved.
6. **Security was flat.** The Defense-controlled apparatus held 12.4% of credits used; its spending was mainly payroll. The presidency grew 26%; the Legislative Assembly almost vanished from the budget.
7. **New instruments were created for the next downturn.** The income-tax reform (September), the state-owned Central Bank (April and December) and loan-financed extraordinary budgets (airport) would change how the State managed the coffee cycle from 1962 on.

## 9. Reading 1961 Against the Hypotheses

**Two interpretations of the spending increase** (project author's framing). The rise in spending while revenue fell can be read as (a) a counter-cyclical stance to support the economy, or (b) a political choice reflecting the Junta's composition — civilian professionals linked to the opposition and to the University (Fabio Castillo Figueroa, René Fortín Magaña, Ricardo Falla Cáceres), with the former University rector Romeo Fortín Magaña among the coup's civilian organizers (Turcios, *Rebelión*, pp. 306–307). The composition of the increase — payroll and transfers, with investment cut — fits (b) better than a classic stimulus. Whether the economy needed support cannot be judged without 1960–62 GDP.

| Hypothesis | 1961 evidence | Verdict |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **H1** Education and the University squeezed in downturns | Education +18.6% (record 21.1% share); Educación subsidies, where the University is probably paid, +72% | **Rejected for 1961** — expansion, not squeeze |
| **H2** Wage containment, especially teachers | Education wages +19.9%; total payroll +3.7%; Defense wages +3.6% | **Rejected for 1961**; posts vs. salary per post open |
| **H3** Public investment cut first | Capital credits −15%; public works −4%; carry-forwards halved | **Supported** (as in 1959) |
| **H4** Monetary tightening | Not observable in the Memoria; Central Bank nationalized in April | **Open** — BCR data needed |
| **H5** IRA food subsidies cut | IRA guarantee fell ₡5.99M → ₡4.34M; budget transfer not identified | **Open; weak sign of a smaller operation** |

**What 1961 sets up.** The regime entered 1962 without a buffer, with a larger and more rigid payroll, and with teachers and the University more dependent on the budget than ever — the precondition of the project's thesis that later adjustment would turn them against the regime. Coffee prices fell another 7% in 1962.
