# El Salvador: Fiscal Diagnostic and Vulnerability Assessment, 1961

**Technical Note — A Revenue Shock Met with Expansion: The Buffer Is Spent (November 2026)**

> **About this note.** This is the first technical diagnostic for 1961. All figures come from the scanned *Informe Complementario Constitucional* for 1961 (signed 28 February 1962 by Minister Humberto Bernal h.). Every figure was read on the page image and checked by arithmetic: revenue subclasses, the ramo totals of credits used and the class columns of Estado 5 all add exactly to the printed totals. 1960 comparisons come from the revised 1960 notes. The budget balance is reported on a commitment basis and an approximate cash basis, as in the other diagnostics.
>
> Pillars (author's definitions): development = education + health + public works; security = Defensa Nacional + Seguridad Pública (the Defense-controlled apparatus); agriculture and Interior are reported separately.

---

## Executive Summary

- **Revenue fell 7.3%.** Rentas dropped ₡12.5M to ₡158.1M, and total income was 5.4% below the budget estimate. Import duties fell 17.1% and explained 73% of the gross decline; coffee export duties fell 12.6%. The income tax rose 11.9%.
- **Spending rose anyway.** Credits used (gastos + compromisos) rose 4.7% to ₡182.5M and payments (gastos) rose 7.6%, the first increase since 1958. The increase was concentrated in education, whose credits used rose 18.6%.
- **The deficit widened sharply.** The commitment-basis result was **−₡24.4M (−15.4% of revenue)**, the largest of the series so far; the approximate cash-basis result was **−₡27.2M (−17.2%)**.
- **The buffer was spent.** The Superávit Financiero fell from ₡20.3M to **₡0.5M**. The Treasury also converted state-held securities into cash (authorized up to ₡22.44M), borrowed from other funds (₡6.2M) and let unpaid legalized orders rise by ₡5.2M. The Memoria describes this as a deliberate "restriction of payments" by priority.
- **Investment absorbed the squeeze.** Capital credits used fell about 15% (₡18.6M → ₡15.9M) and public works fell 4.1%, while education rose 18.6%. The Carretera del Litoral, financed outside the ordinary budget, kept spending (₡12.7M in 1961).
- **Composition: development up, security flat.** Development took **46.5%** of credits used (45.7% in 1960) and security **12.4%** (12.6%). The presidency (Directorio Cívico Militar) rose 25.8%.
- **Direct debt was stable** (₡36.1M, 22.8% of revenue), but **state guarantees rose ₡13.3M to ₡195.8M** (124% of revenue), led by the Acajutla port authority (+₡12.7M).

Overall: **a fiscal stance that turned expansionary in a revenue downturn, financed by exhausting the accumulated surplus, running down liquid assets and delaying payments.** The government entered 1962 with no buffer.

Currency: US$1.00 = ₡2.50, fixed.

The Memoria attributes the shortfall against the estimate almost entirely to import duties (₡11.53M of the ₡13.65M of tax shortfalls). Against 1960, import duties explain 73.1% and export duties 20.3% of the gross revenue decline.

### 2.2 What drove the fall: a puzzle for the coffee-cycle analysis

The world coffee price fell only modestly in 1961. In the project's commodity workbook (World Bank Pink Sheet, annual average), coffee went from US$0.92/kg in 1960 to US$0.90/kg in 1961 (−3%), and to US$0.83/kg in 1962. A 3% price fall does not account for a 12.6% fall in coffee duties and a 17.1% fall in import duties. Possible explanations, none of which the Memoria states:

- **Crop timing and volumes.** The coffee export duty was collected on shipments; a smaller or later-shipped 1960/61 crop would lower duties more than prices.
- **Import compression after the October 1960 and January 1961 coups.** Political uncertainty could have cut imports, and with them the main tax base.
- **Industrial-promotion exemptions and Central American tariff changes.** The new Ley de Fomento Industrial (D. 64, 18 January 1961) renewed and broadened customs franchises. Central American free trade was also expanding.

These hypotheses need BCR data on import values, coffee export volumes and prices, and the balance of payments for 1960–62.

### 2.4 The income tax

Within it, the income tax proper (code 041) was ₡15.41M (₡13.44M in 1960, +14.7%) and the road tax on capital (Vialidad Serie "A", code 045) ₡3.67M (₡3.62M) (Estado 3). The Memoria does not split the income tax between individuals and companies; that requires the Dirección General de Contribuciones Directas or the BCR. The income and road tax together rose ₡2.02M (+11.9%) to ₡19.08M, close to its 1959 peak (₡19.71M), and explained 52.1% of all revenue increases. The rise preceded the income-tax reform of the Directorio Cívico Militar (D. 300, 18 September 1961), which raised marginal rates to a maximum of 76.5% for individuals and 15% for companies and taxed undistributed profits (Pleites, pp. 202–203). Since the tax was assessed on the previous year's income, the reform's yield would appear from 1962. A second law (D. 442, 6 December 1961) then exempted reinvested industrial profits (Pleites, p. 205).

The Memoria is explicit about how the gap was covered:

- **Asset sales.** "To cover the financial needs of the year, the conversion into cash of securities acquired by the State was authorized, up to ₡22,440,000.00."
- **Inter-fund borrowing.** The General Fund borrowed from other funds; the liability reached ₡6.2M (+₡1.7M).
- **Payment arrears as cash management.** The ₡5.2M rise in unpaid legalized orders "means a restriction of payments in certain items of current liabilities, in order to meet others of greater importance or priority" (MH 1961, pp. 27–28).

Current assets fell ₡16.7M and current liabilities rose ₡7.1M. The Superávit Financiero, the accumulated buffer that had absorbed the 1958–59 deficits, was used up in one year.

## 8. Assessment

**Overall.** 1961 is the first year in which a revenue downturn met a fiscal *expansion*. In 1958–59 the Lemus government let the surplus absorb the first year of falling revenue and then cut spending, mainly public works and defense. In 1961 the Junta's budget, executed by the Directorio Cívico Militar, raised education spending by almost a fifth while revenue fell 7%. The data show:

- **The shock came through trade taxes, with imports leading.** Import duties fell more than coffee duties, and both fell more than the world coffee price. The income tax rose. The drivers need BCR trade and coffee data.
- **Education was protected and expanded; investment paid.** Education took 21.1% of credits used, the highest share so far. Capital allocations fell about 15% and public works 4%. Security was flat as a share of credits used.
- **The deficit was financed by liquidating the State's balance sheet, not by borrowing.** The accumulated surplus went from ₡20.3M to ₡0.5M. Securities were sold, inter-fund loans rose and payments were deliberately delayed. Direct debt barely moved.
- **The State's off-budget exposure kept growing.** Guarantees rose to 124% of revenue, now led by the Acajutla port.
- **The government entered 1962 without a buffer.** Any further revenue weakness (coffee prices fell again in 1962) would have to be met by cuts, new taxes or borrowing. The income-tax reform of September 1961 and the nationalization of the Central Bank (April 1961) were the new instruments available.
