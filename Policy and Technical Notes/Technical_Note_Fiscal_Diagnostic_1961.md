# El Salvador: Fiscal Diagnostic and Vulnerability Assessment, 1961

**Technical Note — A Revenue Shock Met with Expansion: The Buffer Is Spent (November 2026)**

> **About this note.** Fiscal diagnostic for 1961, the year of the Junta de Gobierno's budget and the Directorio Cívico Militar's execution. It follows the structure of the diagnostic series for 1952–1960. All figures come from the scanned *Informe Complementario Constitucional* for 1961 (signed 28 February 1962 by Minister Humberto Bernal h.); 1959–60 comparisons come from the revised 1960 notes. Every figure was read on the page image and checked by arithmetic. The budget balance is reported on a commitment basis and an approximate cash basis.
>
> Pillars (author's definitions): development = education + health + public works; security = Defensa Nacional + Seguridad Pública (the Defense-controlled apparatus); agriculture, Interior and the presidency are reported separately.

---

## Executive Summary

**The fiscal position is assessed as "low debt, high stress, high vulnerability."** Stress rose sharply: the budget moved from near-balance to the largest deficit of the series and the accumulated surplus was used up in one year. Debt stayed low because the deficit was financed by running down assets, not by borrowing.

- **Revenue fell 7.3%.** Rentas dropped ₡12.5M to ₡158.1M, 5.4% below the budget estimate. Import duties fell 17.1% and explained 73% of the gross decline; coffee export duties fell 12.6%. The income tax rose 11.9%.
- **Spending rose anyway.** Credits used (gastos + compromisos) rose 4.7% to ₡182.5M, and payments 7.6%. The increase was concentrated in education (+18.6%).
- **The deficit widened sharply:** −₡24.4M (−15.4% of revenue) on a commitment basis, about −₡27.2M (−17.2%) on a cash basis.
- **The buffer was spent.** The Superávit Financiero fell from ₡20.3M to ₡0.5M. The Treasury also sold state-held securities (authorized up to ₡22.44M), borrowed from other funds and let unpaid legalized orders rise by ₡5.2M.
- **Investment absorbed the squeeze.** Capital credits used fell about 15% and public works 4.1%. The IBRD-financed Litoral highway, outside the ordinary budget, accelerated.
- **Composition:** development 46.5% of credits used (45.7% in 1960); security 12.4% (12.6%); presidency +25.8%.
- **Direct debt stable** at ₡36.1M (22.8% of revenue); **state guarantees ₡195.8M** (124% of revenue), led by the Acajutla port authority.

Currency: US$1.00 = ₡2.50, fixed.

**A puzzle for the coffee-cycle analysis.** The world coffee price fell only modestly in 1961: in the project's commodity workbook (World Bank Pink Sheet, annual average) it went from US$0.92/kg in 1960 to US$0.90/kg (−3%), and to US$0.83/kg in 1962. A 3% price fall does not explain a 12.6% fall in coffee duties and a 17.1% fall in import duties. Possible explanations, none stated by the Memoria: crop timing and volumes (the duty was collected on shipments); import compression after the October 1960 and January 1961 coups; and new customs franchises under the Ley de Fomento Industrial (D. 64, 18 January 1961) and Central American free trade. These need BCR data on imports, coffee exports and the balance of payments.

The income and road tax was the only major tax to rise, and explained half of all revenue increases. The rise came before the Directorio's income-tax reform (D. 300, 18 September 1961), which raised marginal rates to a maximum of 76.5% for individuals and 15% for companies and taxed undistributed profits (Pleites, pp. 202–203). Since the tax was assessed on the previous year's income, the reform would yield from 1962. A second law (D. 442, 6 December 1961) exempted reinvested industrial profits (Pleites, p. 205).

## 8. Assessment

**Overall: low debt, high stress, high vulnerability.** This is a shift from 1960's "recovery, near-balance": the stress rose because revenue fell while spending rose, and the accumulated surplus was exhausted in one year.

- **The shock came through trade taxes, with imports leading.** Import duties fell more than coffee duties, and both fell more than the world coffee price. The income tax rose.
- **A revenue downturn met a fiscal expansion.** The Junta's budget raised education by almost a fifth, and the Directorio executed it in full (97% of authorized credits). Two readings are possible: a counter-cyclical stance, or a political choice reflecting the Junta's composition and its debt to the student-led rebellion of 1960. The composition — payroll and transfers up, investment down — fits the second better than a classic stimulus, but GDP data are needed to judge the first.
- **Investment paid.** Capital allocations fell about 15% and public works 4%, while the IBRD-financed Litoral highway accelerated outside the ordinary budget.
- **The deficit was financed by liquidating the State's balance sheet, not by borrowing.** Securities were sold, inter-fund loans rose and payments were delayed. The government entered 1962 with no buffer, and coffee prices fell again in 1962.
