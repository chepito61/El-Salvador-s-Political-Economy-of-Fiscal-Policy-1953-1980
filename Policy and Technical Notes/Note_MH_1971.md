**Working Note — Evidence from the Informe Complementario Constitucional (November 2026)**

**Political economy reading of 1971.**

- **The downturn arrived as the hypothesis predicts.** Coffee fell 13.5% and the export duties 21%. The Government answered in June with a freeze on "non-priority" spending. The teachers struck that summer, for a salary scale that the boom of 1970 had not given them.
- **The cost of adjustment fell on the teachers, and the State answered with force.** The strike was declared illegal and broken by the National Guard, with deaths. In the same budget the Fuerza Armada grew 15.6% and Defensa 19.5%. Basic-education teaching grew 6.5%. Repression rather than negotiation is Almeida's "threat" mechanism, and it is visible in the budget.
- **The State did not cut investment; it borrowed.** Instead of cutting the capital budget, the Government issued ₡25M of domestic bonds and drew on new external loans, chiefly for the education reform (AID, IBRD) and roads (BCIE, Eximbank). Capital credits used almost doubled. This is a new pattern: in the 1966–68 downturn investment was cut first (H3). In 1971 it was protected by debt.
- **Electoral polarization begins.** The 1972 election pitted Molina against a united opposition (UNO). The kidnapping of Regalado Dueñas marks the start of armed action against the coffee elite. **The fiscal squeeze, the strike and the repression all fall in the year before the election that the opposition later called stolen.**

Currency: US$1.00 = ₡2.50.

## 1. Revenue Structure and Performance

### 1.1 Revenue

**Current revenue was ₡299.75M, +6.5%,** but 6.5% below the estimate (₡320.73M). The Memoria (p. 22) blames two items:

- export duties, 74.2% of estimate, because of the coffee price;
- the stamp tax, 60.9% of estimate, because collection did not respond to the 1970 rate increase.

**Other operating income was only ₡0.35M** (the last drawing of the malaria loan), against ₡19.65M of bonds, land and loans in 1970. **Capital revenue was ₡58.81M,** of which ₡46.07M was "accrued" loans and bonds not yet received (section 6).

**Three findings stand out:**

- **Coffee took ₡10.2M out of revenue.** The export duties fell 21% on a 13.5% price fall: the progressive 1950 scale works on the way down too.
- **The gap was filled by taxes on consumption and transactions.** The stamp tax doubled (+₡10.4M) after the September 1970 rate increase. Excises on manufactured goods and consumption rose ₡12.5M. Together they added ₡22.9M, more than twice the coffee loss.
- **The income tax rose 14%,** the first real increase since 1967. The Memoria attributes part of the revenue gain to "improvements in tax administration". Without Estado II-5 the split between persons and companies cannot be checked.

**Customs fell back to 35.6% of revenue,** and to 30.6% without the CACM stabilization tax, the lowest of the period.

**The commitment-basis deficit is not comparable with earlier years.** From 1971 (D.L. 448, December 1971), capital commitments financed with loans and bonds were booked together with an equal amount of "accrued" capital revenue. That inflated credits used by about ₡37M of loan-financed projects not yet under way.

**The comparable reading:**

- **Excluding the loan-financed commitments, the deficit was about ₡19M (6.5% of revenue),** three times the 1970 underlying deficit.
- **On a gastos basis the budget was still in surplus (₡5.2M),** less than half the 1970 surplus.
- **The swing came from two sides:** coffee revenue fell ₡10M while investment, financed by new bonds, rose.

**The Memoria presents 1971 as balanced** (a "superávit presupuestal" of ₡3.28M). It counts ₡58.81M of loans and bonds, ₡46.07M of them not yet received, as revenue. Its conclusion (p. 24) says that "public spending was kept at a level compatible with resources" because of the June measures.

### 7.3 What the war cost the 1971 budget

**The direct cost was about ₡1.8M of interest (0.6% of revenue),** plus ₡1.0M of principal. Small in itself, but it arrived as coffee fell.

**The war's legacy was the debt and a habit:**

- **War bonds of ₡31.8M were still outstanding.** No Dignity bond had been repaid, and they carried ₡1.2M of interest a year.
- **Central Bank financing became a standing facility.** The 1969 advances were repaid in 1970 and 1971, but a new ₡10M advance replaced the last one, so the Treasury's debt to the Central Bank never fell below ₡10M.
- **Interest's share rose.** Total interest and commissions were ₡5.32M, against ₡3.06M in 1969. About a third was war debt.

**Not in the budget again:**

- the returnees from Honduras, now in a second year without a programme;
- the loss of the Honduran market, which became effective in early 1971. It is part of the industrial slowdown that the Memoria blames on exemptions.

## 9. Preliminary Observations

1. **The coffee cycle turned down.** The price fell 13.5% and the export duties 21%; revenue missed its estimate by 6.5%.
2. **The revenue loss was replaced by taxes on consumption and transactions.** The stamp tax doubled and excises rose 19%. The income tax recovered 14%.
3. **A spending freeze in June hit current spending.** Operating credits used grew 2%.
4. **Investment was protected by borrowing.** Capital credits used doubled with ₡25M of domestic bonds and new foreign loans. Direct debt rose ₡32.6M (+24%), the largest increase in colones in the series.
5. **The teachers struck for 53 days and were repressed.** Basic-education teaching grew 6.5%; the Fuerza Armada 15.6%; Defensa 19.5%.
6. **The deficit grew but was disguised.** On a comparable basis it was about 6.5% of revenue. The Memoria reported a surplus by counting loans as revenue.
7. **The Central Bank advance was rolled over,** not repaid.

## 10. Reading 1971 Against the Hypotheses

| Hypothesis | 1971 evidence | Verdict |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **H1** Education and the University squeezed | Education +14.4%; University current +11% plus capital; share up to 27.4% | **Not supported in totals.** The increase was in buildings and the reform (foreign-financed), not in teachers |
| **H2** Wage containment, especially teachers | Payroll +7.7%; basic-education teaching +6.5%; Fuerza Armada +15.6%; June freeze; strike declared illegal and repressed; a salary-scale law passed | **Supported.** The containment was enforced by force, not only by budget |
| **H3** Public investment cut first | Capital credits used +92%, financed by bonds and loans; operating spending frozen | **Not supported in 1971:** a change from 1966–68. Borrowing replaced cuts. It shifts the adjustment to later years (debt service) |
| **H4** Monetary policy | Central Bank advance repaid and immediately renewed (₡10M); ₡25M of domestic bonds authorized | **Accommodation continued.** Central Bank credit became a permanent facility |
| **H5** IRA food subsidies cut | Paid support ₡1.52M (+9%); ₡7.30M of bonds issued for past losses | **Not cut,** but small. The quasi-fiscal losses are now Treasury debt |
| *Track A: tax bargain* | Coffee duty down; stamp tax ×2; excises +19%; income tax +14% (split unknown); exemptions blamed for customs decline | **The adjustment fell on consumers and transactions,** not on coffee incomes or industrial profits |
| *Track B: safety valve* | Honduras leaves the CACM; still no budget line for returnees | **Closed, with no fiscal response** |
| *Track C: protection bargain* | Defensa +19.5%; Fuerza Armada +15.6%; National Guard breaks the strike; Presidency discretionary +7.4% | **Defensa grew faster than any large civilian ramo except Agriculture and Economía** |
| *Track D: reform diluted* | Education reform imposed from above with AID and IBRD loans; the teachers' alternative plan rejected | **A reform implemented, but against its own implementers** |

**The story in one paragraph.** In 1971 the coffee cycle turned down, and the budget showed what the regime did in a bust after a boom. Revenue missed its target by 6.5% as coffee fell. The Government froze current spending in June, as the teachers walked out for a salary scale that the 1970 windfall had not given them. It broke the 53-day strike by force, while the army's budget grew more than twice as fast as teaching. It did not cut investment. It borrowed, at home and abroad, and direct debt rose by a quarter. It kept its Central Bank credit line open. It filled the revenue gap with stamp and consumption taxes, not with taxes on coffee or profits. And it reported a surplus. **The pattern for 1971 is "contain the teachers, protect the investment with debt, tax consumption".** That shapes 1972, an election year in which the united opposition was denied the presidency and the Army occupied the University.
