# Loan-Financed Investment and the Ordinary Budget, 1955–1962

**Working Note — El Salvador's Political Economy of Fiscal Policy, 1953–1980 (November 2026)**

> **Purpose.** This note opens a new line of analysis proposed by the project author from professional experience: in Central America, infrastructure is financed mainly through loans and grants from multilateral and bilateral lenders (IBRD, IDB, USAID and its predecessors, Japan, France). These operations sit outside the ordinary budget, in extraordinary budgets or autonomous agencies, but they form a **de facto capital-investment budget that interacts with the ordinary operating budget**: to execute them the government must find counterpart funds, service the debt, and later staff and maintain the assets. All figures come from the Memorias de Hacienda (MH) 1961 and 1962 and were checked on the page images. The data are in "Loans and Investment/loan_financed_projects_1955_1962.csv".

---

## 1. The three channels of public investment

| Channel | Where it appears | Examples, 1955–1962 |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **Ordinary budget** | Gastos de Capital (Equipo, Propiedad) in the General Fund, mainly Obras Públicas | Roads, buildings, equipment |
| **Extraordinary budgets** (loan- or grant-financed, administered by the Treasury) | Separate Estados 24–28 of each Memoria | Carretera del Litoral (IBRD, from 1954); Ilopango airport (Eximbank + DLF, from 1961); school buildings and teacher training (US grant, Alliance for Progress, 1962); PL-480 agricultural products agreement (US credit, 1961–62) |
| **Autonomous agencies with state guarantees** | Estado 21, *garantías subsidiarias del Estado* | CEL (electricity), CEPA (Acajutla port), Banco Hipotecario, IRA, IVU and BCR (IDB loans, 1962) |

The pillar analysis so far has used only the first channel. The second and third grew while the first was cut.

## 2. The Carretera del Litoral: how a loan-financed project used fiscal space

The Litoral highway (D.L. 1702, December 1954) is the first large IBRD-financed project. The 1962 Memoria gives its full financing and spending history.

**Financing of the project, 1955–1962 (cumulative):** General Fund ₡50.60M (56.6%); IBRD loans ₡37.31M (41.8%); fines assigned to the fund ₡1.41M (1.6%).

**Debt service on the Litoral loans** (paid from the ordinary budget's Deuda Pública ramo):

|:--|--:|--:|

| Interest, main-road loan | 0.93 | 0.80 |
| Interest, access-roads loan | 0.14 | 0.37 |
| **Debt service** | **4.30** | **4.54** |
| **Counterpart + debt service** | **8.20** | **7.74** |

| *Memo: ordinary-budget capital payments (Equipo + Propiedad)* | *11.72* | *8.81* |

### What this shows

- **The "foreign-financed" road was mostly financed at home.** Over eight years the General Fund paid 57% of the project's cost; the IBRD loan covered 42%.
- **The counterpart competed with ordinary public works.** In 1957–58 the counterpart was equal to about 30% of the Ministry of Public Works' entire allocation. It stayed high in 1958 (₡9.7M), the first year of the coffee downturn, and fell in 1959 when public works was cut by a fifth.
- **The fiscal cost continued after construction.** By 1961–62, debt service on the Litoral loans was ₡4.3–4.5M a year. Together with the counterpart it took about 5% of revenue, close to the whole ordinary-budget capital spending (₡8.8M in 1962).

## 3. 1961–1962: the second channel widens

| Project | Legal basis | Financing | Status at end-1962 |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Ilopango airport modernization | D. 487 (22 Dec 1961) | Eximbank US$2.65M + DLF US$1.6M (₡10.63M); General Fund ₡0.21M | ₡0.35M spent; **₡9.71M committed** |
| School buildings and teacher training | D.L. 51 and 81 (1962) | US grant, Alliance for Progress, ₡5.25M | ₡1.01M received; ₡0.27M spent, ₡0.88M committed |
| Agricultural Products Agreement (PL-480) | D. 283 (1961); D. 58 (1962) | US credit from the sale of farm surpluses; ₡10.52M budgeted | ₡1.46M received; ₡1.64M lent to the Administración de Bienestar Campesino |
| IBRD access roads to the Litoral | Litoral budget | IBRD loan | ₡3.97M (1961) and ₡4.21M (1962) disbursed |

In 1962 the ordinary budget's capital credits were ₡10.3M. Commitments on the airport alone were ₡9.7M. The extraordinary, externally financed budget was becoming as large as the ordinary investment budget.

## 4. How to track it from 1963 onward

For each year, the analysis will add a **consolidated capital-investment table** with:

1. Ordinary-budget capital spending (Equipo + Propiedad), by ramo.
2. Spending, commitments and financing of each extraordinary budget (loan, grant, General Fund counterpart).
3. New loans contracted and disbursed, by lender (IBRD, IDB, USAID/AID, DLF, Eximbank, CABEI, others), from Estados 19–20.
4. Debt service on project loans.
5. State guarantees by agency (CEL, CEPA, ANDA, IVU, Banco Hipotecario, IRA).

Three indicators will connect it to the operating budget and to the pillars:

- **Counterpart burden** = General Fund contributions to loan projects / revenue.
- **Project debt service** / revenue.
- **Consolidated investment** = ordinary capital + extraordinary-budget capital, and the share financed abroad.

The project register in "Loans and Investment/" will be extended year by year. The question for the thesis is whether, in coffee downturns, the counterpart and debt-service obligations of externally financed projects squeezed the operating budget (teachers, the University, health), or whether those projects were themselves delayed.
