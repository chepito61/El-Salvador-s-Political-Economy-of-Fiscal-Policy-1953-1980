# El Salvador: Fiscal Diagnostic and Vulnerability Assessment, 1969

**Technical Note — A War Financed Off the Ordinary Budget: Emergency Tax, Patriotic Bonds and Central Bank Notes (November 2026)**

> **About this note.** This is the fiscal diagnostic for 1969, the year of the war with Honduras. It follows the structure of the diagnostic series for 1952–1968.
>
> **Sources.** The *Informe Complementario Constitucional* 1969:
> - Estado II-5 (revenue);
> - Estado II-7 (spending by unit and class, gastos basis);
> - Estado II-8 (credits used by ramo, 1967–1969);
> - the debt statements.
>
> Every figure was read on the page image and checked by arithmetic.
>
> **Pillars.** Development = education + health + public works. Security = Ramo de Defensa including the police. From 1968 security is also shown including the extraordinary Defence budget and, in 1969, the emergency provision.

---

## Executive Summary

**The fiscal position is assessed as "rising debt, high stress, high vulnerability".** The war opened the largest deficit of the decade. It was financed with domestic debt and Central Bank credit. Ordinary finances improved.

- **Ordinary revenue rose 5.1% to ₡242.7M.**
  - The emergency tax added ₡9.3M (₡252.0M in total).
  - The CACM stabilization tax added ₡10.7M.
  - The coffee duty rose 12.1% at a flat price.
  - Without the stabilization tax, customs would have fallen to 31.8% of revenue.
- **Credits used rose 24.5% to ₡291.9M,** including ₡41.0M of emergency (war) spending. Of the emergency spending paid in 1969, 90% was machinery and equipment.
- **The deficit (commitment basis) was −₡39.9M, −15.9% of revenue,** the largest of the 1960s. Excluding the war: −₡8.2M (−3.4%).
- **The Superávit Financiero turned negative (−₡9.9M) for the first time since 1962.** Treasury notes held by the Central Bank doubled to ₡20M.
- **Composition (credits used):**
  - development 45.2% (+11.5% in money);
  - ordinary security 9.7%;
  - **security including the emergency provision 23.7%.**
- **Debt.** Direct debt ₡125.6M (+₡25.8M; 52% of revenue). Internal debt doubled to ₡44.0M, 72% of it for military purposes. Guarantees ₡190.5M.

Currency: US$1.00 = ₡2.50.

| Export duties (coffee) | 13.7% | 10.6% | 11.3% | CRITICAL |
| Consumption taxes | 24.8% | 24.9% | 25.0% | MODERATE |
| Income tax | 17.0% | 19.6% | 15.7% | VOLATILE (one-off transfers) |
| Property and transfers | 5.1% | 9.1% | 10.2% | RISING |
| Other taxes and non-tax | 13.2% | 12.9% | 14.2% | MODERATE |

**The revenue base is weak once the one-off items are removed.** Excluding the withholding transfers of 1967–68 and the stabilization tax of 1969, ordinary revenue rose from ₡224.5M (1965) to about ₡232M (1969), only 3–4% in four years. Over the same period current spending rose 19.6%, excluding the emergency (Memoria, p. 39).

*Excluding the stabilization tax, import duties fell 26% between 1966 and 1969. The 1969 Memoria again attributes the decline "especially [to] the granting of fiscal incentives to firms that have received benefits under the Ley de Fomento" (p. 28).*

**A legacy of the 1950 coffee regime.** The Interior budget still pays the municipalities ₡0.35M "to compensate for the suppression of taxes on the cultivation of coffee and for other municipal activities". This is the compensation created by D. 836 (1950), which replaced local coffee taxes with the export tax.

**The Memoria itself identifies the structural squeeze.** Current revenue rose 8.1% from 1965 to 1969 while current spending (excluding the emergency) rose 19.6%. "The operating saving destined for the Capital Budget has been gradually shrinking, making the financing of the Central Government's investments more difficult" (p. 39). Investment in 1969 was restored partly with bonds rather than with saving.

## 9. Assessment

**Overall: rising debt, high stress, high vulnerability.**

- **The war was financed off the ordinary budget.**
  - ₡41M of emergency credits, 90% of the paid part on equipment, were covered by a one-time tax, patriotic bonds and Central Bank notes.
  - Development spending was not cut; investment was restored.
  - The fiscal cost of the war was moved into debt and into monetary financing.
- **The ordinary structure kept weakening.**
  - Customs revenue is held up by a temporary regional tax, and the operating saving keeps shrinking.
  - Underlying revenue grew 3–4% over 1965–69, against 19.6% for current spending.
- **Monetary accommodation was explicit.** The Treasury's notes are now stated to be held by the Central Bank, and doubled to ₡20M.
- **The war's social cost was not budgeted.** There is no fiscal line for the 60,000–130,000 returnees. The food subsidy (IRA) kept falling.
