# El Salvador: Fiscal Year 1978 — The Coffee Bust, a Deficit of a Fifth of Revenue, and Security First

**Working Note — Evidence from the Informe Complementario Constitucional (November 2026)**

> **About this note.** This is the working note on the 1978 Memoria, in the format of the 1952–1977 series. It ends with sections on the cost of the war and on the hypotheses (H1–H5) and the four tracks of the *Political Economy Context* note. Every figure was read on the page image and checked by arithmetic. The IMF-style diagnostic is in the companion *Technical Note 1978*.
>
> **What this volume contains.** The 1978 Memoria (73 scanned pages) has the narrative (Cuadros 1–24), the revenue statements (Estados I-4 and II-1, 1974–1978), **the debt statement by instrument (Estado III-9)**, and **Estado IV-2: spending by unit for the 1977 budget, liquidated at 31 December 1978**. So this note gives the **1977 unit lines**; the 1978 unit lines are expected in MH 1979.
>
> **Revisions that change earlier notes.** MH 1978 revises the public debt series for 1974–1977 (Cuadro 24, "cifras revisadas"): the Jiboa sugar-mill financing (₡75.5M) is shown as **external non-guaranteed bank credit to INSAFI**, not as guaranteed internal bonds. It also revises GDP and therefore the tax burden for 1975–1977. The 1976 and 1977 notes carry dated corrections.

**Political economy reading of 1978.**

- **The coffee bust hit the budget in one year.** Current revenue fell 13% (to ₡1,026.9M), the coffee duty 70%. The Memoria blames "the changing and therefore difficult economic situation".
- **Spending did not adjust; its composition did.** Credits used still rose 7.5%. **Defensa rose 37% and Seguridad Pública 19%; Education fell 0.7%** in nominal terms. The security share of credits used reached 9.2%, the highest in the 1973–1978 series.
- **The comparable deficit reached a fifth of revenue** (−₡190.4M, −18.5%), and **the Treasury went back to the Central Bank** (₡8.0M of advances) and to foreign loans (₡93.9M used).
- **Hidden arrears surfaced.** The State owed the Social Security Institute ₡60M of unpaid contributions up to 1977 and paid them with bonds. The 1977 surplus had been flattered by these unpaid bills.
- **The tax bargain moved a little, too late.** Company income tax rose 39% and a new agricultural land tax yielded ₡10.1M, but neither came close to replacing the coffee duty.
- **The Presidency's lump sum grew again in the year of the inauguration** (1977: ₡12.8M of non-personal services, +83%), while the University's real subsidy recovered only partly.

Currency: US$1.00 = ₡2.50.

## 9. Preliminary Observations

1. **Coffee price −30%, coffee duty −70%; current revenue −13%.**
2. **Credits used +7.5%; the comparable deficit reached 18.5% of revenue** (12.7% excluding the ISSS arrears), the worst of the series.
3. **Security +32% (Defensa +37%); Education −0.7%.** Security reached 9.2% of credits used.
4. **₡60M of unpaid State contributions to Social Security came to light,** paid with bonds.
5. **Central Bank advances again (₡8.0M);** direct debt +37%, above 60% of revenue.
6. **The paper surplus fell to ₡10.7M;** without the unsold bonds, −₡224M.
7. **Company income tax +39% and a new land tax (₡10.1M):** too small and too late to offset coffee.
8. **1977 unit lines:** teachers +1% real; University +7% real; Fuerza Armada +15% real; Presidency lump sum +83%; ISTA −61%.

## 10. Reading 1978 Against the Hypotheses

| Hypothesis | 1978 evidence (and 1977 unit lines) | Verdict |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **H1** Education and the University squeezed | Education −0.7% nominal (about −12% real) in 1978; University subsidy +7% real in 1977 | **Education squeezed in the bust; the University recovered partly in 1977** |
| **H2** Wage containment, especially teachers | Teachers' line +1% real (1977); remunerations +4% real (1978) | **Teachers' real pay held flat;** the wage bill grew with employment |
| **H3** Public investment cut first | Capital budget −13%; capital commitments −28%; real investment −3%; security +32% | **Yes: investment was cut first, security protected** |
| **H4** Monetary policy | Central Bank advances ₡8.0M; external loans ₡111M; ISSS paid in bonds; inflation 13.3% | **Central Bank financing resumed** (small); the State financed itself through arrears and bonds to its own institutions |
| **H5** IRA food subsidies cut | IRA ₡24.0M in 1977 (+22% real); ₡11.95M of IRA storage loans in 1978 | **Expanded** |
| *Track A: tax bargain* | Company income tax +39%; agricultural land tax (₡10.1M) | **A first move toward taxing land and profits, at the bottom of the cycle** |
| *Track B: safety valve* | War bonds: Defence bonds repaid; Dignity bonds untouched | **Closed** |
| *Track C: protection bargain* | Security +32%; IACHR visit; Public Order law in force; Presidency lump sum ₡12.8M (1977) | **Security first in the bust** |
| *Track D: reform diluted* | ISTA −61% (1977); agrarian bonds unused | **Agrarian reform defunded** |

**The story in one paragraph.** In 1978 the coffee price fell by 30% and the progressive coffee duty by 70%. Revenue fell 13%, but spending did not: credits used rose 7.5%, and the comparable deficit swung from a surplus of 8% of revenue to a deficit of 18.5%, the worst of the series. The adjustment fell on investment and on Education, which fell in nominal terms, while Defence rose 37% and the security share of the budget reached its highest level of the decade. The bust also exposed what the boom had hidden: ₡60M of contributions the State had not paid to Social Security, settled with bonds, and a paper surplus that still counted ₡235M of bonds no one had bought. The Treasury went back to the Central Bank and to foreign lenders; direct debt rose 37%. A new land tax and higher company income tax arrived only at the bottom of the cycle, and the agrarian institute's funding was cut by three-fifths. **The pattern for 1978 is "revenue falls, security rises, education and investment pay, and the State borrows from its own institutions".**
