# El Salvador: Fiscal Year 1964 — The Coffee Recovery, the New Income Tax and Foreign Loans Inside the Budget

**Working Note — Evidence from the Informe Complementario Constitucional (November 2026)**

> **About this note.** Working note on the 1964 Memoria, in the format of the 1952–1963 series, with a final section that reads the year against the project's hypotheses (H1–H5). Every figure was read on the page image and checked by arithmetic. Political periods follow Roberto Turcios: 1964 belongs to **authoritarian reformism**, in its institutional phase under the 1962 Constitution and the PCN government (*Rebelión*, pp. 325–326). ## Introduction

The scanned 1964 volume has 94 pages: the explanatory note and narrative (printed pp. 15–33), Estados II-4 and II-5 (revenue against estimates, and revenue for 1962–64), Estado II-7 (spending by organizational unit, programme and class) and Estados VII-1 and VII-2 (public debt and its service). **The Estados on commitments by ramo, on earlier budgets and on the extraordinary and special funds are not reproduced.** Their totals come from the narrative.

**1964 is a break in the series.** Three changes took effect at once:

- **A new budget structure.** Spending is split into a *Presupuesto de Funcionamiento* (operating programmes, current transfers, pensions, debt) and a *Presupuesto de Capital* (investment programmes and capital transfers). The classes change too: *Gratificaciones*, *Equipo* and *Propiedad* disappear; *Maquinaria y Equipo*, *Construcciones*, *Inmuebles* and *Desembolsos Financieros* appear.
- **Foreign loans now enter the budget.** AID programme loans (₡7.75M) are recorded as *Ingresos de Capital* and spent through the capital budget. Until 1963, loan-financed works ran in extraordinary budgets outside the General Fund.
- **The income tax is split for the first time** into individuals, companies and other income.

Because commitments by ramo are not published, **the ministry comparisons in this note use payments (gastos), not credits used.** The 1963 comparison is recomputed on the same basis.

## 1. Revenue Structure and Performance

### 1.1 Revenue growth

Current revenue (*ingresos corrientes*, the equivalent of the earlier *rentas*) was **₡210,458,487.47**, up ₡29.9M. That is **+16.6%** on the 1963 rentas (₡180.53M) and +16.2% on the Memoria's restated 1963 figure (₡181.13M), which is the rate printed in the text. It was the largest increase of the series. Capital revenue added ₡7,769,497.37, almost all AID loan drawings (₡7,749,400.56), for a total of **₡218,227,984.84**.

Current revenue was 3.1% above its estimate (₡204.09M). Total income was 2.7% below the overall estimate (₡224.28M) because the AID loans were drawn more slowly than budgeted (p. 21).

*\*1963: ordinary capital credits (Equipo + Propiedad), not strictly comparable. Sources: MH 1964, pp. 21–28; Estados II-5, II-7, VII-1 and VII-2.*

**What changed in 1964 is where the loans sit.** The AID programme loans signed in September 1963 were placed inside the General Budget: drawn as capital revenue (₡7.75M) and spent as capital transfers and loans to the rural-credit, livestock, irrigation and industrial-credit agencies. Three of these lines (livestock, soil conservation, INSAFI) equal the AID drawings to the cent, and the ABC loan matches within 1%; the school-construction drawing (₡0.75M) has no single matching line. The Memoria says so itself: "the movement of capital revenue tends to modify the traditional structure of the State's general revenue and, consequently, the trend of public spending" (p. 23).

The roads programme and airport still ran outside the budget, so the 1964 Informe does not report their spending. **The roads counterpart lagged.** The General Fund paid ₡0.4M to the Programa Nacional de Carreteras against a schedule of about ₡3.4M a year. Further sums sit in the capital-transfer commitments (₡4.12M).

## 8. Preliminary Observations

1. **The coffee upswing and the tax reform arrived together.** Current revenue rose 16.6% and passed the 1957 peak. The new income tax and the coffee duty each supplied about a quarter to a third of the increase.
2. **The income tax is split for the first time:** companies about 40%, individuals about 50%.
3. **The budget closed in surplus on a commitment basis** (+₡5.9M) for the first time since 1957. The Superávit Financiero rose to ₡13.9M, and the BCR notes were paid off.
4. **Spending grew about half as fast as revenue** (gastos +8.7%). The government saved a large part of the windfall.
5. **Teachers' pay moved again** (+7.0% for primary salaries), while total permanent salaries rose 1.0%.
6. **AID loans entered the budget,** doubling Agriculture's spending and opening a capital budget of ₡33.5M (16% of credits used).
7. **Security fell slightly as a share** (11.4% of gastos). The police line grew on paper through what appears to be a reclassification.
8. **Debt rose 21%** on loan drawings, and guarantees grew through IDB lending to housing and water. Large undrawn balances (about ₡52M) were already committed.

## 9. Reading 1964 Against the Hypotheses

1964 is the first **upswing** year of the coffee cycle in this series since 1957. The hypotheses concern downturns, so the year serves as a control: what did the regime do with a windfall?

| Hypothesis | 1964 evidence | Verdict |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **H1** Education and the University squeezed | Education +6.4%; share held (23.3% excluding AID pass-through). University ₡4.61M, including its first capital transfer (₡0.8M) | **Not supported in 1964** |
| **H2** Wage containment, especially teachers | Primary salaries +7.0% after +1.5%; total permanent salaries +1.0% | **Not supported for teachers in the upswing.** Whether the rise is posts or pay per post needs the budget law |
| **H3** Public investment cut first | Capital-budget direct investment ₡9.2M (vs. ₡7.5M ordinary capital in 1963); capital transfers ₡24.4M; drawings ₡16.0M; roads counterpart in arrears | **Not supported in the upswing;** investment shifted further to transfers and loans |
| **H4** Monetary tightening | Treasury repaid the BCR notes (₡6M) and built cash. No new Central Bank credit to government | **No tightening by the Treasury;** the boom was used to retire monetary financing |
| **H5** IRA food subsidies cut | IRA received ₡0.84M (subvención ₡0.63M + subsidy ₡0.21M), the first year it is visible by beneficiary; its guarantee is no longer reported | **Open;** first data point for the series |

**The story in one paragraph.** In 1964 the coffee price recovered and the income-tax reform took hold, and revenue jumped 17%. The Rivera government spent about half of the increase:

- it raised education and teachers' pay;
- it gave the University its first capital grant;
- it held defence nearly flat.

It saved the rest, ending with a surplus and no Central Bank debt. It also changed how infrastructure is financed: AID programme loans entered the budget as capital revenue and left as capital transfers to credit and development agencies, while the IDB lent to housing and water agencies under State guarantee. **The upswing did not polarize through the budget; it bought room.** The test of the hypotheses is whether the next coffee downturn reverses the 1964 gains for teachers and the University while the counterpart and debt-service obligations built up in 1963–64 stay fixed.
