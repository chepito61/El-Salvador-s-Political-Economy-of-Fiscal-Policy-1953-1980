# El Salvador: Fiscal Year 1963 — Rivera's First Full Year: Consolidating Central Bank Credit and Building Roads with Foreign Loans

**Working Note — Evidence from the Informe Complementario Constitucional (November 2026)**

> **About this note.** Working note on the 1963 Memoria, in the format of the 1952–1962 series, with a final section that reads the year against the project's hypotheses (H1–H5). Every figure was read on the page image and checked by arithmetic. Political periods follow Roberto Turcios: 1963 belongs to **authoritarian reformism**, now in its institutional phase under the 1962 Constitution and the PCN government (*Rebelión*, pp. 325–326). The IMF-style diagnostic is in the companion *Technical Note 1963*.

## Introduction

The scanned 1963 volume has 85 pages: the narrative (printed pp. 9–61, Sections I–VII), Estados N° 2 and 3 (revenue), Estado N° 5 (spending by organizational unit and class, with a summary by ramo), Estado N° 7 (1963 payments against the 1962 budget, by unit and class), and Estados N° 19–21 (public debt, debt service, state guarantees). **Estados N° 1, 4, 6, 8–18 and 22–29 are not reproduced**; their totals come from the narrative.

From 1963 the budget uses a new grouping of spending: *Gastos de Funcionamiento* (operation and equipment), *Gastos de Capital–Propiedad*, *Transferencias* (*subvenciones*, *subsidios* and a new class of *aportes*), *Clases Pasivas*, *Deuda Pública* and *Egresos que no representan gastos*. Estado 5 keeps the old class columns.

## 1. Revenue Structure and Performance

### 1.1 Revenue growth

Rentas were **₡180,525,607.75**, up ₡11,921,919.29 (**+7.1%**). With *otros ingresos* (₡672,517.89), total income was ₡181,198,125.64, ₡12.80M (6.6%) below the budget estimate. The largest shortfall was in the income tax (₡5.31M), followed by import duties (₡2.49M), *especies fiscales* (₡2.57M) and export duties (₡1.68M).

**The tendency the project author identified is now explicit.** In 1963:

- Extraordinary, foreign-financed budgets spent ₡12.2M — 1.6 times the ordinary budget's own capital spending (₡7.5M of credits used).
- The ordinary budget financed infrastructure mainly through counterpart *aportes* (₡2.3M received by the programmes, plus ₡1.9M reserved for the roads programme) and through debt service on the Litoral loans (₡5.5M).
- The roads programme alone locks ₡13.75M of General Fund counterpart into the budgets of 1963–66, about ₡3.4M a year.

## 8. Preliminary Observations

1. **Revenue grew for the second year,** now led by consumption taxes, including two new taxes (refined petroleum and flour). Trade taxes fell to 45.9% of revenue.
2. **The income tax stalled after the reform year** (+5.2%) and was the largest shortfall against the estimate (₡5.3M).
3. **The deficit narrowed to 3% of revenue,** and the financial position returned to a small surplus — thanks to the conversion of ₡10M of BCR credit into bonds, which the Memoria counted as a resource.
4. **Payroll growth slowed sharply** (+1.1%), and teachers' salaries rose only 1.5% after 13.6% in 1962.
5. **Ordinary investment kept falling** (−27%), while the foreign-financed roads programme, airport and schools spent 1.6 times as much. Public works rose as *aportes* to these programmes, not as direct construction.
6. **Security was flat** (12.2% of credits used); its equipment remained small. The presidency's 1962 increase was reversed.
7. **The State's commitments widened:** debt rose 34%, guarantees rose ₡8.9M through IDB lending to the Central Bank and housing institute, and ₡13.75M of counterpart was committed to 1966.

## 9. Reading 1963 Against the Hypotheses

| Hypothesis | 1963 evidence | Verdict |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **H1** Education and the University squeezed | Education +4.3% (22.1%); likely University line +28%, reversing most of the 1962 cut | **Not supported in 1963** |
| **H2** Wage containment, especially teachers | Payroll +1.1%; primary salaries +1.5% after +13.6% | **Supported in a mild form** — the first year of near-freeze for teachers |
| **H3** Public investment cut first | Ordinary capital −27% (third consecutive cut); replaced by foreign-financed programmes | **Supported for the ordinary budget; qualified by the shift to loans** |
| **H4** Monetary tightening | BCR credit converted into ₡10M of long-term bonds; ₡6M of notes still owed | **Not tightening — consolidation of monetary financing** |
| **H5** IRA food subsidies cut | IRA guaranteed credit −₡1.9M (to ₡5.7M) after +₡3.3M in 1962 | **Open; consistent with a smaller operation in 1963** |

**The story in one paragraph.** In its first full year, the Rivera government consolidated the adjustment of 1962. It did not raise the income tax further, but added consumption taxes; it held payroll almost flat; it turned Central Bank credit into long-term debt; and it moved infrastructure into foreign-financed programmes, paying for its share through counterpart *aportes* and debt service. Education held its share and the likely University line recovered — the year of the University reform. **The fiscal pressure on teachers appears for the first time as wage restraint (+1.5%) rather than cuts.** Whether this persists, and whether the counterpart obligations of the roads programme compete with education in the next coffee downturn, is the question for 1964 onward.
