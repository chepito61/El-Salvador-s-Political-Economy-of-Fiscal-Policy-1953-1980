# El Salvador: Fiscal Year 1966 — The First Downturn Year: Wages Protected, Investment Cut, the Buffer Spent

**Working Note — Evidence from the Informe Complementario Constitucional (November 2026)**

> **About this note.** Working note on the 1966 Memoria, in the format of the 1952–1965 series, with a final section that reads the year against the project's hypotheses (H1–H5). Every figure was read on the page image and checked by arithmetic. Political periods follow Roberto Turcios: 1966 belongs to **authoritarian reformism** under the Rivera government. The IMF-style diagnostic is in the companion *Technical Note 1966*.

---

## Political Context

President: Colonel Julio Adalberto Rivera (PCN), 1 July 1962–1 July 1967. The Minister of Finance, Alvaro Marino, signed the Informe on 28 February 1967. **1966 is the first year of a two-year (biennial) budget for 1966–1967.**

**The Rivera administration in context.** The government is usually described as reformist. It benefited from coffee prices without wild downward swings, and from the employment and growth that import-substituting industrialization (ISI) and the Central American Common Market (CACM) brought. In 1966 the coffee price fell for the first time since 1963. The Memoria itself names both sides of the regional model as causes of weak revenue: coffee and cotton prices, and the customs revenue lost to regional integration and to the incentives of the Ley de Fomento Industrial.

| Date | Event | Source |
|:--|:--|:--|
| 1966 | Coffee price falls 7% (US$1.00 → US$0.93/kg); cotton suffers "adverse effects" | Pink Sheet; MH 1966, p. 22 |
| 11 Mar 1966 | AID loan of ₡3.0M for 12 rural roads | MH 1966, Estado VII-1 |
| Mar 1966 | Legislative and municipal elections. The PCN keeps its majority, and José Napoleón Duarte (PDC) is re-elected mayor of San Salvador | Election records |
| 25 Mar 1966 | D.L. 555 creates a *Fondo Especial de Inversiones para el Desarrollo Económico y Social* (₡6M of Treasury cash lent to IVU, ANDA, IRA, ICR, the Feria and municipalities) | MH 1966, p. 31 |
| 5 May 1966 | IDB loan of ₡8.1M for the Acajutla port extension (guaranteed; undrawn) | MH 1966, Estado VII-1 |
| 1966 | Honduran landowners form FENAGH and press for protection from *campesinos*, many of them Salvadoran migrants. Honduras's 1962 land reform will be enforced against Salvadoran occupants from 1967 | Football War background (Wikipedia) |
| 29 Oct 1966 | AID loan of ₡4.09M for malaria eradication | MH 1966, Estado VII-1 |

**The Honduras question.** By 1969 more than 300,000 Salvadorans lived in Honduras, over 10% of its population. Their displacement is the other face of El Salvador's land scarcity and of the CACM's unequal gains. It becomes a fiscal and security issue from 1967 onward, and the notes will follow it.

Currency: US$1.00 = ₡2.50 (colones), fixed exchange rate.

## 1. Revenue Structure and Performance

### 1.1 Revenue

Current revenue was **₡223,519,277.91**, down ₡1.02M (**−0.5%**) and ₡17.0M (7%) below its estimate of ₡240.5M. It is the first fall since 1961. Capital revenue was ₡8.67M, of which AID loan drawings ₡8.16M, against ₡17.48M in 1965 and an estimate of ₡16.68M. Total income was **₡232,192,622.09** (−4.1%).

**The two motors stalled together:**

- **The coffee duty fell 9.6%** on a 7.1% fall in the world price.
- **Import duties did not grow** even though imports were expanding. The Memoria explains this by "the process of regional economic integration and in particular, the application of the incentives of the Ley de Fomento Industrial to a larger number of firms" (p. 22). Integration and import substitution moved imports into tariff-free regional trade and exempt industrial inputs.

## 8. Preliminary Observations

1. **The first downturn of the Rivera years.** Revenue fell 0.5% against an expected rise of 7%. Coffee fell, and import duties stagnated under regional integration and industrial incentives.
2. **The company income tax fell for the second year**, to ₡9.6M, a third below 1964. Individuals now pay 57% of the income tax.
3. **Operating spending rose 7.6% and the payroll 10.2%;** capital spending was cut 9.3%. The commitment-basis deficit reached 11.9% of revenue.
4. **The whole 1965 surplus was used.** The Superávit Financiero fell to zero, and the Treasury borrowed ₡9.6M from other funds.
5. **Education (+8.0%), the University (+14.3%) and health (+14.5%) were protected.** Security was flat, and its share fell to 10.4%, the lowest of the series.
6. **The presidency grew 17%,** with ₡0.66M of unitemized transfers in the *Administración Particular*.
7. **Guarantees rose 20%, to 70% of revenue.** The Litoral loan was paid off, but new AID and IDB loans were signed.

## 9. Reading 1966 Against the Hypotheses

1966 is the **first downturn year** since the cycle turned: the coffee price fell 7% and revenue was flat. This is the first real test of H1–H5.

| Hypothesis | 1966 evidence | Verdict |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **H1** Education and the University squeezed | Education +8.0%; University +14.3% (₡8.0M); primary +7.5% | **Not supported** in the first downturn year |
| **H2** Wage containment, especially teachers | Payroll +10.2%; permanent salaries +5.4% "as social welfare policy"; primary personal services ₡31.1M | **Not supported:** wages were protected and expanded |
| **H3** Public investment cut first | Capital spending −9.3%; capital transfers −30.6%; Public Works −7.3%, Agriculture −6.7%, Economía −23.3%; operating +7.6% | **Supported:** the adjustment fell on investment, partly through slower loan drawings |
| **H4** Monetary tightening | No Central Bank credit; surplus exhausted; Treasury borrowed ₡9.6M from other funds | **No monetary tightening;** the Treasury's own liquidity was squeezed |
| **H5** IRA food subsidies cut | IRA ₡0.91M (+13.1%) plus Fondo Especial loans | **Not supported** |

**The story in one paragraph.** When coffee turned down in 1966, the Rivera government did not squeeze education, teachers or the University. It raised the payroll 10%, the University's grant 14% and health 15%. It cut investment and spent the entire 1965 surplus, ending the year with no financial buffer and ₡9.6M owed to its own funds.

Two structural weaknesses surfaced at the same time:

- **The ISI–CACM model was eroding the customs base and the company income tax.** Industrial incentives and regional free trade lowered import duties and corporate taxes.
- **Loan-linked commitments kept growing.** Guarantees reached 70% of revenue.

**The regime's reformism was financed by running down reserves, not by taxing the beneficiaries of the boom.** If the downturn continues into 1967, the government will have no surplus left, so the H1–H2 squeeze, if it comes, should show up there. 1967 is also the year of the first ANDES strike and of the full enforcement of Honduras's land reform against Salvadoran migrants.
