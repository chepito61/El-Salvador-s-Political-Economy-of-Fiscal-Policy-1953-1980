# El Salvador: Fiscal Year 1965 — Spending Catches Up with the Boom: The Capital Budget Doubles on Foreign Loans

**Working Note — Evidence from the Informe Complementario Constitucional (November 2026)**

> **About this note.** Working note on the 1965 Memoria, in the format of the 1952–1964 series, with a final section that reads the year against the project's hypotheses (H1–H5). Every figure was read on the page image and checked by arithmetic. Political periods follow Roberto Turcios: 1965 belongs to **authoritarian reformism**, in its institutional phase under the 1962 Constitution and the PCN government (*Rebelión*, pp. 325–326). The IMF-style diagnostic is in the companion *Technical Note 1965*.

## Introduction

The scanned 1965 volume has 94 pages and follows the 1964 format:

- the introduction and narrative (printed pp. 15–33);
- Estados II-4 and II-5 (revenue against estimates; revenue for 1962–65);
- Estado II-7 (spending by unit, programme and class);
- Estados VII-1 and VII-2 (debt and debt service for 1962–65).

**Two kinds of material are missing.** The Estados on commitments by ramo, earlier budgets and extraordinary funds are not reproduced. One page of debt service, with the direct external loans one by one (printed pp. 399–401), is also missing.

**Basis of comparison.** As in 1964, ministry comparisons use **payments (gastos)**. 1964 and 1965 are now on the same budget structure, so the year-on-year comparison is clean.

## 1. Revenue Structure and Performance

### 1.1 Revenue growth

Current revenue was **₡224,542,101.52**, up ₡14.08M (**+6.7%**) and ₡8.71M (4%) above its estimate. Capital revenue was ₡17,479,212.02, almost all AID loan drawings (₡17,440,040.89), against ₡27.97M expected. Total income was **₡242,021,313.54**.

**Two things changed under the surface:**

- **The coffee duty rose 18% with no help from the price.** The Pink Sheet price was flat (US$1.01 → US$1.00/kg, −0.9%), so the gain came from export volume and timing.
- **Petroleum shifted from an import tax to a consumption tax.** The consumption tax on petroleum products more than doubled (+₡7.45M) while import duties on fuels fell by ₡5.51M. The net gain was ₡1.94M. This is consistent with crude being refined in the country rather than importing products.

**The mechanism the project author identified is now the core of public investment:**

- **Foreign loans financed a third of the capital budget.** The AID programme loans flow through the budget to the credit agencies (ABC, INSAFI, META, MEGA).

## 8. Preliminary Observations

1. **Revenue kept growing (+6.7%) on coffee volume and consumption taxes.** The coffee price stopped rising, and the company income tax fell 19%.
2. **Spending outran revenue:** gastos +15.9%, capital spending +70%. The commitment-basis result returned to deficit (−6.4% of revenue), financed by foreign loans and the operating saving.
3. **The 1964 surplus was spent on wages, the University and municipalities** through an additional credit of ₡7.94M.
4. **The University's transfer rose 52%,** to ₡7.0M. Primary teachers' salaries rose only 2.6%, against 5.5% for all permanent salaries. ANDES 21 de Junio was founded in June.
5. **Security grew 10.7% but fell as a share** (10.9% of gastos). The Guardia Nacional appears by name in the accounts for the first time.
6. **Debt rose 27% and guarantees 14%,** with about ₡28M of undrawn direct loans still to come.
7. **The presidency's *Administración Particular*** (₡1.62M, including ₡0.61M of unitemized transfers) is now the line to follow for discretionary funds.

## 9. Reading 1965 Against the Hypotheses

1965 is the **peak** of the coffee cycle in this decade. The price held at its 1964 level and fell in 1966 (US$0.93/kg). The regime used the peak to expand spending, especially capital spending financed with foreign loans.

| Hypothesis | 1965 evidence | Verdict |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **H1** Education and the University squeezed | Education +14.2%; University +52% (₡7.0M), including ₡1.5M capital | **Not supported;** the University's best year so far |
| **H2** Wage containment, especially teachers | Permanent salaries +5.5% (general increase of ₡1.78M); primary salaries +2.6% (+5.3% including Escuelas Renovadas); ANDES founded in June | **Partly supported:** teachers' pay grew more slowly than the government average, the year teachers organized |
| **H3** Public investment cut first | Capital spending +70%; capital budget 23% of credits used | **Not supported in the peak year** |
| **H4** Monetary tightening | No Central Bank credit; cash up to ₡11.0M; financing came from foreign loans | **No tightening by the Treasury** |
| **H5** IRA food subsidies cut | IRA ₡0.81M (−4.2%) | **Marginal cut;** open |

**The story in one paragraph.** At the top of the coffee cycle, the Rivera government spent the windfall:

- a general wage increase;
- 52% more for the University;
- doubled transfers to housing and water agencies;
- a capital budget that grew 70% and was a third financed by AID and IDA loans.

The budget returned to a commitment-basis deficit, covered by loans rather than Central Bank credit. Teachers did less well than the rest of the public payroll, and in June they founded ANDES. The ingredients for the next phase are in place: spending commitments, counterpart obligations and debt service locked in at peak revenue, just before the coffee price turns down in 1966–67. **The test of H1–H3 is now whether the downturn falls on teachers and the University while the loan-linked commitments are protected.**
