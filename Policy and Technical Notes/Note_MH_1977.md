**Political economy reading of 1977.**

- **The coffee peak.** Current revenue rose 46%, to ₡1,181.8M; the coffee duty alone rose ₡189.7M. The Memoria calls the five-year revenue growth (30.5% a year) "without precedent in the fiscal history of the country".
- **For the first time in the series the comparable balance was in surplus,** +₡98.5M, 8.3% of revenue. Credits used rose only 21%, half as fast as revenue. The Treasury's cash rose to ₡137.2M, and it repaid the Central Bank's advances. **The windfall was finally saved, in the year it peaked.**
- **The saving was channelled through a State lending fund.** ₡100M more went to the Fund for Economic Development Financing, which lent to autonomous institutions instead of their issuing guaranteed bonds. The coffee company's loan from the General Fund was back to ₡30.0M.
- **The security apparatus grew fastest.** Defensa +39.5% and Seguridad Pública +42.0%, in the year of the fraud, the Plaza Libertad shootings, the state of siege, the killing of priests and the Public Order law. Development grew 23%.
- **Coffee paid at the border, not on profits.** Company income tax rose 7.5% in the most profitable coffee year on record; it was 13% of the coffee duty.
- **The agrarian programme shrank.** The Agriculture ramo fell 10%. The World Bank later reports that the new administration redirected land policy to a land bank (COFINTA) that would buy and resell land.

Currency: US$1.00 = ₡2.50.

- **Direct debt fell to 39.5% of current revenue** (53.8% in 1976). No bonds were sold; ₡20.85M of internal bonds were repaid.
- **Guaranteed internal bonds fell 19%.** The Memoria explains why: autonomous institutions placed fewer bonds because **the Central Government lent to them through the Special Fund for Economic and Social Development Investment.** The 1976 shift to guarantees was partly reversed through Treasury lending.
- **Debt service** (interest ₡17.70M; amortization ₡29.32M) was ₡47.02M, **4.0% of current revenue.**

## 9. Preliminary Observations

1. **Revenue +46% to ₡1,181.8M; coffee duty ₡416.8M, 35% of revenue,** the peak of the series.
2. **Company income tax +7.5%,** 13% of the coffee duty.
3. **First comparable surplus of the series: +₡98.5M (+8.3% of revenue);** the Treasury repaid the Central Bank and accumulated cash.
4. **A ₡100M development lending fund** replaced guaranteed bond issues by autonomous institutions; the coffee company's loan went back to ₡30M.
5. **Security +40% (Defensa +39.5%, Seguridad Pública +42%)** in the year of the fraud, the state of siege and the Public Order law.
6. **Agriculture −10%;** the agrarian reform's financing was not renewed.
7. **The paper surplus still includes ₡237M of unsold bonds;** open commitments reached ₡513M.
8. **The University's subsidy fell 8% in real terms over 1974–76;** a ₡6.2M increase was voted in 1977.

## 10. Reading 1977 Against the Hypotheses

| Hypothesis | 1977 evidence | Verdict |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **H1** Education and the University squeezed | Education +23% (credits used); University subsidy −7.6% real over 1974–76 (final figures); +₡6.2M voted in 1977 | **University squeezed through 1976; partial catch-up in 1977** |
| **H2** Wage containment, especially teachers | Remunerations +16.8% (+4.5% real); teachers' line +45.9% over 1974–76 (+14.5% real) | **No containment of the wage bill;** real growth slowed sharply in 1977 |
| **H3** Public investment cut first | Capital budget +22%; real investment +18% | **Investment continued to grow,** more slowly |
| **H4** Monetary policy | Central Bank advances: +₡12.0M in 1976, repaid in 1977; no bond sales; Treasury cash +₡60M; inflation 11.8% | **Central Bank financing in 1976 (small), reversed in 1977;** the Treasury became a net lender |
| **H5** IRA food subsidies cut | IRA ₡17.7M in 1976 (final); in 1977, +₡0.7M of subsidy and ₡4.6M to amortize IRA's internal debt (additional credits); General Fund loan ₡2.8M | **Maintained** |
| *Track A: tax bargain* | Coffee duty ₡416.8M; company income tax +7.5%; amnesty (D. 248) raised personal and capital taxes | **The windfall was taxed at the border; profits again largely untouched** |
| *Track B: safety valve* | No war-related flows identifiable in the 1977 volume | **Closed** |
| *Track C: protection bargain* | Security +40%; fraud, state of siege, killings of priests, UGB ultimatum, Public Order law; coffee company loan ₡30M | **The protection bargain hardened into repression** |
| *Track D: reform diluted* | Agriculture −10%; no ISTA extraordinary contribution; land policy redirected to a land bank (COFINTA, World Bank 1979) | **Reform abandoned; replaced by market-based land purchase** |

**The story in one paragraph.** In 1977 coffee peaked, and so did the State's coffee revenue: the duty brought in ₡417M, more than a third of revenue. For the first time in the series the budget was in comparable surplus, and the Treasury used the windfall to repay the Central Bank, cut its arrears, build up cash and lend ₡100M more to public institutions through a development fund. But 1977 was also the year the regime closed the political system: an election denounced as fraudulent, the Plaza Libertad shootings and a state of siege, the killing of two priests, the White Warriors' ultimatum to the Jesuits, and a Public Order law that criminalized dissent. Defence and public security spending grew 40%. Profits from the coffee boom were barely taxed, the coffee company's loan from the Treasury went back to ₡30M, and the agrarian reform was replaced by a land bank. **The pattern for 1977 is "save the windfall, tax coffee only at the border, lend to the State's own enterprises, and fund the repression".**
