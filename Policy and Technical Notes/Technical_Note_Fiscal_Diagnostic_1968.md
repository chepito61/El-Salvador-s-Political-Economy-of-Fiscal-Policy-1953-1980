# El Salvador: Fiscal Diagnostic and Vulnerability Assessment, 1968

**Technical Note — Investment Halved, Arrears to Social Security, Defence Financed Off-Budget (November 2026)**

> **About this note.** This is the fiscal diagnostic for 1968, the first annual budget of the Sánchez Hernández government after the 1966–67 biennium. It follows the structure of the diagnostic series for 1952–1967.
>
> **Sources.**
> - The *Informe Complementario Constitucional* 1968.
> - For spending by ramo, Estado II-8 of the 1969 Informe: credits used (gastos plus commitments) for 1967 and 1968, from one table. The 1968 table by unit is not in the scans.
>
> Every figure was read on the page image and checked by arithmetic.
>
> **Pillars.** Development = education + health + public works. Security = Ramo de Defensa, including the police; from 1968 it is also shown including the extraordinary Defence budget.

---

## Executive Summary

**The fiscal position is assessed as "moderate and rising debt, high stress, high vulnerability".** The recorded deficit narrowed again, but only because the capital budget was halved. Large obligations, mainly to the social security institute, were left off the books.

- **Revenue +1.8% to ₡231.0M,** 3.8% below estimate.
  - The coffee duty fell 21.5% at a flat price.
  - Customs fell to 33.5% of revenue.
  - Growth came from the Vialidad tax (nearly tripled) and from wage withholding credited to the income tax.
- **Spending.** Gastos −2.0% to ₡222.0M; credits used −1.0% to ₡234.4M.
  - Capital budget −52% (gastos), −42% (credits used).
  - Operating budget +6.9%.
- **Recorded deficit −₡3.4M (−1.5% of revenue).** Adding ₡16.9M of unrecorded obligations gives about −₡20M (−8.8%).
- **Superávit Financiero ₡0.11M.** Treasury notes rose from ₡6M to ₡10M.
- **Composition (credits used):**
  - education 26.7% (+9.2%); health 15.2% (+10.9%); public works 8.5% (−26.1%);
  - security 10.9% on the ordinary budget;
  - **14.5% including the ₡9.8M extraordinary Defence budget financed by bonds.**
- **Debt.** Direct debt ₡99.7M (43% of revenue), up ₡10.8M, of which ₡10M were military bonds. Guarantees ₡181.6M (79% of revenue).

Currency: US$1.00 = ₡2.50.

| Other taxes and non-tax | 12.5% | 13.2% | 12.9% | MODERATE |

**The revenue structure changed more in 1968 than in any year since 1964.** Border taxes fell from 39.6% to 33.5% of revenue, and direct taxes rose from 22.1% to 28.7%.

- **Not all of the direct-tax gain will recur.** The ₡6.59M of wage withholding credited in 1968 included balances held over from earlier years (MH 1969, p. 27).
- **Collection was weak.** 1,700 taxpayers deferred ₡13.9M of income and Vialidad tax.

## 8. Assessment

**Overall: moderate and rising debt, high stress, high vulnerability.**

- **Third year of adjustment through investment.** The capital budget fell by half and public works by a quarter. What remained of investment was the education reform (television, the technical institute) and hospitals.
- **The revenue base shifted inward.** Coffee and customs fell; property (Vialidad) and wage taxes rose. Taxes moved toward salaried earners and property owners. Companies' contribution stayed flat, and coffee's fell with the border duty.
- **Arrears replaced the formal deficit.** The recorded deficit of 1.5% of revenue hides about ₡17M of unpaid and unrecorded obligations. The largest was ₡10.2M owed to the ISSS.
- **Military spending moved off-budget.** A ₡10M bond-financed extraordinary budget raised security spending by a third, a year before the Honduras war. The ordinary security share understates the State's priorities from 1968 on.
