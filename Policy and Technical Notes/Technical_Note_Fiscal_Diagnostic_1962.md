# El Salvador: Fiscal Diagnostic and Vulnerability Assessment, 1962

**Technical Note — Adjustment Without a Buffer: New Taxes, Central Bank Credit and Investment Cuts (November 2026)**

> **About this note.** This is the first technical diagnostic for 1962. All figures come from the scanned *Informe Complementario Constitucional* for 1962 (signed 28 February 1963 by Minister Alvaro Marino). Every figure was read on the page image and checked by arithmetic: the revenue subclasses, the ramos of credits used, the class columns of Estado 5 for each pillar ramo and unit, the guarantees and the Treasury obligations all add exactly to the printed totals. 1961 comparisons come from the revised 1961 notes. The budget balance is reported on a commitment basis and an approximate cash basis.
>
> Pillars (author's definitions): development = education + health + public works; security = the Defense-controlled apparatus. **From 1962 the police are a unit of the Ramo de Defensa** ("Cuerpos de Seguridad Pública"); the separate Ramo de Seguridad Pública disappears. Security is therefore Defensa alone from 1962, comparable with Defensa + Seguridad Pública before.

---

## Executive Summary

- **Revenue recovered 6.7%, driven by the income-tax reform.** Rentas rose ₡10.5M to ₡168.6M. The income and road tax jumped ₡7.7M (+40.5%) to ₡26.8M, the first yield of the 1961 reform. Coffee export duties fell again (−8.2%). Total income was still 6.4% below the budget estimate, again mainly because of import duties.
- **Spending was held flat.** Payments (gastos) rose only 0.2% and credits used fell 1.3%, to ₡180.0M.
- **The deficit narrowed but remained large:** **−₡11.4M (−6.8% of revenue)** on a commitment basis and about **−₡13.6M (−8.1%)** on a cash basis.
- **The surplus turned into a deficit.** The General Fund ended the year with a **Déficit Financiero of ₡6.9M**, after starting with a surplus of ₡0.5M.
- **The newly nationalized Central Bank financed the Treasury.** The General Fund owed **₡15.5M in promissory notes (*pagarés*) to the Banco Central de Reserva**, its largest short-term obligation. Borrowing from other funds rose to ₡7.3M. Unpaid legalized orders were cut from ₡9.1M to ₡5.0M.
- **Investment took the cut.** Capital credits used fell 35% (₡15.9M → ₡10.3M) and public works 17%. Aid-financed investment began outside the ordinary budget: the US Alliance-for-Progress school-building grant, the Eximbank/DLF airport loans and the PL-480 agreement.
- **Composition: education protected, security flat, the presidency up.** Education rose 2.5% to a record 21.9% of credits used; teachers' pay rose 13%, but Educación's subsidies (where the University is likely paid) fell 22%. Security was flat (−0.7%) at 12.5%. The presidency rose 33%.
- **Guarantees reached ₡200.6M** (119% of revenue). The IRA's guaranteed credit rose 75%.

Overall: **the government closed most of the 1961 gap through new direct taxes and investment cuts, and financed the rest with Central Bank credit — the first use of the monetary instrument created in 1961.**

Currency: US$1.00 = ₡2.50, fixed.

### 2.3 The coffee channel

Coffee export duties fell ₡1.9M (−8.2%) to ₡21.1M, 20% below 1960. The Pink Sheet coffee price fell 7.0% in 1962 (US$0.90 → US$0.83/kg). This time the duty moved with the price. Import duties were again the main shortfall against the estimate (₡12.7M of ₡17.6M of tax shortfalls).

## 8. Assessment

**Overall.** 1962 is the adjustment year that 1961 made inevitable. The government of the Directorio and its successors (provisional president Cordón, then President Rivera from 1 July 1962) adjusted on three fronts at once:

- **New direct taxes.** The income-tax reform raised ₡7.7M in its first year, the largest single change in the revenue base since the coffee boom. Adjustment was partly carried by higher incomes, not only by spending cuts.
- **Investment cuts.** Ordinary-budget investment fell 35% and public works 17%. Payroll was protected: it rose 6.5%, with teachers' salaries up 13.6%.
- **Central Bank credit.** With no surplus left, the Treasury borrowed ₡15.5M through short-term notes from the BCR, nationalized in 1961 (₡14.0M net of the ₡1.5M passed on to the housing institute). It is the first year in the series in which the deficit was financed by the central bank.

Outside the ordinary budget, the Alliance for Progress and multilateral lenders (US grants and PL-480 credit, Eximbank, DLF, IDB, IBRD) became a second channel of public investment.
