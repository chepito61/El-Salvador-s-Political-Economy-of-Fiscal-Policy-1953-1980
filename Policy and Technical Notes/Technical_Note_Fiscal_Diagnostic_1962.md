# El Salvador: Fiscal Diagnostic and Vulnerability Assessment, 1962

**Technical Note — Adjustment Without a Buffer: New Taxes, Central Bank Credit and Investment Cuts (November 2026)**

> **About this note.** Fiscal diagnostic for 1962, the first budget of authoritarian reformism (decreed by the Directorio Cívico Militar, executed under provisional president Cordón and, from 1 July, President Rivera). It follows the structure of the diagnostic series for 1952–1961. All figures come from the scanned *Informe Complementario Constitucional* for 1962 (signed 28 February 1963 by Minister Alvaro Marino); 1960–61 comparisons from the revised notes. Every figure was read on the page image and checked by arithmetic.
>
> Pillars (author's definitions): development = education + health + public works; security = the Defense-controlled apparatus. **From 1962 the police are a unit of the Ramo de Defensa** ("Cuerpos de Seguridad Pública"); the separate Ramo de Seguridad Pública disappears. Security is therefore Defensa alone from 1962, comparable with Defensa + Seguridad Pública before.

---

## Executive Summary

**The fiscal position is assessed as "low but rising debt, high but easing stress, high vulnerability."** The deficit halved, but the government ended the year with a negative financial position and financed itself, for the first time in the series, with Central Bank credit.

- **Revenue recovered 6.7%, driven by the income-tax reform.** Rentas rose ₡10.5M to ₡168.6M. The income tax proper rose 50% (₡15.4M → ₡23.1M), the first yield of the 1961 reform. Coffee export duties fell again (−8.2%). Total income was 6.4% below the budget estimate.
- **Spending was held flat.** Payments rose 0.2%; credits used fell 1.3% to ₡180.0M.
- **The deficit narrowed but stayed large:** −₡11.4M (−6.8% of revenue) on a commitment basis, about −₡13.6M (−8.1%) on a cash basis.
- **The surplus turned into a deficit.** The General Fund ended with a **Déficit Financiero of ₡6.9M**.
- **The Central Bank financed the Treasury.** The General Fund owed **₡15.5M in promissory notes (*pagarés*) to the Banco Central de Reserva**, its largest short-term liability. Payment arrears were reduced.
- **Investment took the cut.** Capital credits used fell 35% and public works 17%. Aid-financed investment (Alliance for Progress grants, PL-480 credit, Eximbank/DLF airport loans) began outside the ordinary budget.
- **Composition:** education rose to a record 21.9% of credits used (teachers' pay +13.6%), but Educación's subsidies — the likely University line — fell 22%. Security was flat (12.5%). The presidency rose 33%.
- **Guarantees ₡200.6M** (119% of revenue); the IRA's guaranteed credit rose 75%.

Currency: US$1.00 = ₡2.50, fixed.

## 8. Assessment

**Overall: low but rising debt, high but easing stress, high vulnerability.** Compared with 1961 ("low debt, high stress, high vulnerability"), the deficit halved and revenue recovered, but the financial position turned negative and debt rose by 43% once Central Bank notes are counted.

- **Adjustment on three fronts.** The government closed most of the 1961 gap with a new direct tax (income tax +50%), investment cuts (ordinary capital −35%, public works −17%) and Central Bank credit (₡15.5M). Payroll was protected: teachers' salaries rose 13.6%.
- **A change in the revenue base.** Revenue grew while coffee duties fell, driven by a direct tax, and trade taxes dropped below half of revenue.
- **Monetary financing began.** The Central Bank nationalized in 1961 was used in its first full year to finance the Treasury.
- **Two investment budgets.** Ordinary investment shrank while a loan- and grant-financed extraordinary budget grew under the Alliance for Progress, with counterpart and debt-service claims on the ordinary budget.
