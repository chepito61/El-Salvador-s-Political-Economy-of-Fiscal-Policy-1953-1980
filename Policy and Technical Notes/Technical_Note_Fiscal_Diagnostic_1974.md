# El Salvador: Fiscal Diagnostic and Vulnerability Assessment, 1974

**Technical Note — Record Revenue, a Spending Surge, and a Comparable Deficit of 9% of Revenue (November 2026)**

> **About this note.** This is the fiscal diagnostic for 1974. It follows the structure of the diagnostic series for 1952–1973.
>
> **Sources.** The *Informe Complementario Constitucional* 1974:
> - the analytical narrative (Cuadros 1–22);
> - Estado I-4 (revenue by item);
> - debt statements VI-1 and VI-2.
>
> **The scan's unit-level spending statement (IV-2) covers the 1973 budget, not 1974.** Ministry comparisons are therefore on credits used (Cuadro 8), for 1973 and 1974. Every figure was read on the page image and checked by arithmetic.
>
> **Pillars.** Development = education + health + public works. Security = Defensa + Ramo de Seguridad Pública.

---

## Executive Summary

**The fiscal position is assessed as "deteriorating underlying balance masked by surpluses, rising debt, high vulnerability (cyclical and political)".**

- **Revenue.** Current revenue +20.3% to ₡484.3M, 18.2% above estimate.
  - Coffee duty ₡90.0M (+21.5%); company income tax ₡32.3M (+57%); stamp tax ₡40.0M (+44%).
  - Tax / GDP 11.4% (Memoria, preliminary).
- **Spending.** Credits used +32.1% to ₡594.5M; gastos +26.8% to ₡509.2M.
  - Capital budget +66%; Defensa +37%; Presidency +52%; Agriculture +68%.
  - Remunerations +18.6% (wage policy against inflation).
- **Deficit.** Commitment basis −₡110.2M (−22.7%). **Underlying −₡43.0M (−8.9%),** up from −2.2% in 1973, the worst since 1966.
- **Liquidity.** Superávit Financiero ₡42.4M, carried by the 1973 surplus and ₡85.4M of accrued receivables (unsold bonds). The ₡54M of bonds authorized were not sold. Central Bank notes ₡1.2M.
- **Debt.** Direct debt +25.1% to ₡323.2M (66.7% of revenue); direct service 5.9% of revenue; total public debt ₡548.3M (13.0% of GDP).
- **Composition (credits used):** development 49.3%; security 8.5%; social development (sectoral) 39.2%, down from 44.2%.

Currency: US$1.00 = ₡2.50.

## 9. Assessment

**Overall: deteriorating underlying balance masked by surpluses; rising debt; high vulnerability (cyclical and political).**

- **Revenue was at a record,** carried by coffee, company profits and the stamp tax.
- **Spending outran it.** Credits used rose 32%, against 20% for revenue, with investment, Defence, the Presidency and agriculture leading. Remunerations rose 18.6% under a wage policy against inflation.
- **The comparable deficit quadrupled to 8.9% of revenue,** but the accounts showed a surplus thanks to the 1973 windfall and unsold bonds.
- **Fiscal risk changed.** The budget is now committed to permanent wage and investment spending that only a continued coffee boom can finance.
