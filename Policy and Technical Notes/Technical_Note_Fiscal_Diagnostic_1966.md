# El Salvador: Fiscal Diagnostic and Vulnerability Assessment, 1966

**Technical Note — The First Downturn: Flat Revenue, Rising Payroll and an Exhausted Buffer (November 2026)**

> **About this note.** Fiscal diagnostic for 1966, the first year of the 1966–67 biennial budget under the Rivera government. It follows the structure of the diagnostic series for 1952–1965. All figures come from the scanned *Informe Complementario Constitucional* for 1966 (signed 28 February 1967 by Minister Alvaro Marino). Every figure was read on the page image and checked by arithmetic. The composition of spending is on **payments (gastos)**, as in 1964–65.
>
> Pillars: development = education + health + public works; security = Ramo de Defensa, including the police (Seguridad Pública).

---

## Executive Summary

**The fiscal position is assessed as "moderate and rising debt, high stress, high vulnerability."** Compared with 1965 ("low-to-moderate stress"), stress rose sharply: revenue fell, the deficit doubled and the financial buffer was used up within the year.

- **Revenue −0.5% to ₡223.5M,** 7% below its estimate. The coffee duty fell 9.6% (price −7.1%). Import duties stagnated, which the Memoria attributes to Central American integration and industrial incentives. The company income tax fell 16.6%, a third below 1964.
- **Spending +4.2%** (gastos ₡232.1M): operating +7.6%, capital −9.3%. Credits used ₡250.1M (+4.7%).
- **Payroll +10.2%** to ₡121.4M: 52.3% of gastos and 54.3% of revenue.
- **Deficit −₡26.6M on a commitment basis (−11.9% of revenue),** the worst since 1961. Only ₡8.4M of it was loan-financed.
- **Superávit Financiero fell from ₡17.9M to ₡0.01M;** the Treasury borrowed ₡9.6M from other funds.
- **Composition (gastos):** development 45.4%, security 10.4% (lowest of the series), education 22.8%.
- **Direct debt ₡82.6M** (36.9% of revenue, +10%); guarantees ₡156.1M (70% of revenue, +20%).

Currency: US$1.00 = ₡2.50, fixed.

### 2.2 The two channels of the downturn

| Channel | 1965 → 1966 | Mechanism |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Coffee | Duty −₡3.46M (−9.6%) on a price fall of 7.1% | The coffee cycle |
| Customs | Import duties flat (−0.1%) | Imports shifted to tariff-free CACM trade and to inputs exempted under the Ley de Fomento Industrial (Memoria, p. 22) |
| Company tax | −₡1.90M (−16.6%), 28% below estimate | Industrial incentives and weak assessment of the new law |
| Offsets | Personal income tax +9.2%; consumption +1.7%; non-tax +9.5% | Broad-based taxes on households |

**The regional development model was eroding the tax base it relied on.** Trade taxes fell to 43% of revenue. The revenue that held up came from individuals and consumers, not from the firms that benefited from integration and incentives.

## 8. Assessment

**Overall: moderate and rising debt, high stress, high vulnerability.**

- **The first downturn of the Rivera years** hit a budget that had grown at peak revenue. Revenue fell while operating spending rose 7.6%.
- **The adjustment fell on investment,** especially transfers to autonomous agencies. Education, health, the University, wages and the presidency were protected or expanded.
- **The buffer was spent in one year.** No surplus remains to cushion a second bad year.
- **The tax base of the ISI–CACM model was weak.** Customs revenue stalled and the company income tax fell a third in two years. Revenue depended on coffee, consumption and individuals.
- **Contingent liabilities reached 70% of revenue,** through the State's guarantees of the autonomous agencies' borrowing.
