# El Salvador: Fiscal Year 1973 — The Coffee Boom Pays Off the Central Bank and Doubles Public Works

**Working Note — Evidence from the Informe Complementario Constitucional (November 2026)**

**Political economy reading of 1973.**

- **The upside of the coffee cycle, again.** Revenue rose 23% and the coffee duty more than half. As in 1970, the windfall went first to cleaning the books: the Central Bank was repaid almost in full. Then it went to public works, which grew by two-thirds, and to agriculture.
- **The teachers' gains were not repeated.** After the 25% raise of 1972, basic-education teachers' pay rose only 5.8% in a year when revenue rose 23%. The pattern of 1970 recurs: **in booms the windfall goes to works and to paying debts, not to the teachers.** The 1968 and 1971 strikes followed such years.
- **The University was refinanced under government control.** Its subsidy rose 59%, but the institution was now run by the Government's appointees. **Money returned after autonomy was taken away.**
- **Security grew more slowly than development for the first time since 1970,** but the Fuerza Armada line grew 31%.
- **The quiet year before the storm.** 1973 had no major national confrontation in the sources consulted. The large works it financed (Cerrón Grande) and the inflation that arrived at its end were the background of the peasant and labour mobilization of 1974–75. **[To document from the 1974 Memoria and the literature.]**

Currency: US$1.00 = ₡2.50.

**Four findings stand out:**

- **Coffee paid for the year.** Export duties rose ₡28.6M, 37% of the whole revenue increase, on a price rise of 24%. Their elasticity to the price was about 2.5, the highest in the series, because the quota limits lapsed as well.
- **The income tax rose 20% for a third year of growth,** to ₡62.6M. **Company income tax was ₡20.5M,** against ₡11.6M in 1970. The Memoria credits the Dirección General de Contribuciones Directas' efforts at "a fair assessment of personal and company incomes".
- **Property and wealth taxes jumped 31%,** but the Memoria calls this "exceptional". It came from the inheritance tax (₡6.7M), "related to the death of wealthy taxpayers".
- **The stamp tax kept growing** (+20%) to ₡27.7M. It has grown 48% a year on average since its 1970 reform.

### 7.3 What the war cost the 1973 budget

**The direct cost was about ₡2.7M (interest plus Defence amortization), 0.7% of revenue.**

- **Cumulative interest on the war bonds (≈₡8.6M) is now almost equal to the emergency tax collected to pay for the war (₡9.6M).** It will exceed it in 1974.
- **The Dignity bonds (₡25.8M) have not been amortized at all.** Nearly nine-tenths of the war's bond finance (₡31.3M of ₡35.8M) is still outstanding five years later.
- **The war-era Central Bank financing was finally cleared in 1973,** by the coffee boom, as in 1970.

## 9. Preliminary Observations

1. **The coffee boom:** price +24%, export duties +60%, revenue +23%, the largest increase in the series.
2. **Company income tax nearly doubled since 1970,** to ₡20.5M; the income tax grew 20%.
3. **The windfall cleared the Central Bank** (₡17.4M to ₡0.7M) and produced a financial surplus of ₡39M.
4. **Public works grew 66%,** agriculture 39% and the University's subsidy 59%. Teachers' pay grew only 6%.
5. **The Presidency's lump sum nearly doubled** to ₡2.2M; the Fuerza Armada line grew 31%; Seguridad Pública fell.
6. **Direct debt rose 26%,** almost all external, to finance the 1973–77 programme of large works.
7. **The war debt is still there:** the Dignity bonds have not been amortized, and cumulative interest is about to exceed the emergency tax.

## 10. Reading 1973 Against the Hypotheses

| Hypothesis | 1973 evidence | Verdict |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **H1** Education and the University squeezed | Education +14%; University current +59% under government control | **Not supported in money.** The University was refunded after being taken over |
| **H2** Wage containment, especially teachers | Teachers +5.8%; payroll +8.7% in a year when revenue rose 23% | **Supported again in the boom:** the 1972 raise was not repeated |
| **H3** Public investment cut first | Public works +66%; capital budget +25%; operating saving finances 70% of capital spending | **Supported in reverse:** investment is the swing variable, up in booms |
| **H4** Monetary policy | Central Bank notes repaid (₡17.4M to ₡0.7M) | **Pro-cyclical repayment:** Central Bank credit expands in busts and is repaid in booms |
| **H5** IRA food subsidies cut | IRA support doubled (₡3.06M) as world grain prices rose | **Not cut;** increased under external price pressure |
| *Track A: tax bargain* | Coffee duty ₡74M; company income tax ₡20.5M; inheritance tax windfall; no reform | **The boom was taxed mainly at the border;** company income tax began to respond |
| *Track B: safety valve* | No budget line for returnees; Cerrón Grande dam financed (displacement to be documented) | **Closed** |
| *Track C: protection bargain* | Fuerza Armada +31%; Presidency lump sum +77%; Seguridad Pública −3% | **Discretionary lump sums at the top grow fastest** |
| *Track D: reform diluted* | University under government administration; no agrarian measure in the budget | **No opening** |

**The story in one paragraph.** In 1973 coffee boomed and the budget showed again what the regime did with a windfall. It repaid the Central Bank. It built: public works grew by two-thirds and the external debt for the large projects of 1973–77 began to accumulate. It refinanced the University it had occupied, now under its own administrators. It did not repeat the teachers' raise of the election year. The Presidency's discretionary lump sum nearly doubled. **The pattern for 1973 is "build with the windfall and borrow for more, repay the Central Bank, keep wages in check".** It is the same pattern as 1970, and it set the scene for the inflation of 1974 that would erode teachers' and workers' real pay.
