# El Salvador: Fiscal Diagnostic and Vulnerability Assessment, 1971

**Technical Note — Coffee Falls; Current Spending Is Frozen, Investment Is Financed by Debt, and the Deficit Is Reported as a Surplus (November 2026)**

> **About this note.** This is the fiscal diagnostic for 1971. It follows the structure of the diagnostic series for 1952–1970.
>
> **Sources.** The *Informe Complementario Constitucional* 1971:
> - narrative;
> - Estado II-7 (gastos by unit and class);
> - debt statements VII-1 and VII-2.
>
> The 1971 scan contains **no Estado II-5 (revenue by item) and no Estado II-8.** Revenue is taken from the narrative's tables. The income-tax split and the coffee duty proper are not available. Ministry comparisons are on the gastos basis (II-7, 1970 and 1971). Every figure was read on the page image and checked by arithmetic.
>
> **Pillars.** Development = education + health + public works. Security = Defensa + Ramo de Seguridad Pública.

---

## Executive Summary

**The fiscal position is assessed as "rising debt, rising stress, high vulnerability (cyclical and political)".** The coffee downturn was met with a current-spending freeze and with borrowing for investment.

- **Revenue.** Current revenue rose 6.5% to ₡299.8M but fell 6.5% short of the estimate.
  - Coffee price −13.5%; export duties −21.1%.
  - Stamp tax ×2.1 and excises +19%, after the 1970 reforms; income tax +14%.
- **Spending.** Gastos +9.3% to ₡294.5M; credits used +17.4% to ₡356.1M.
  - Operating credits used +2.1% after the June 1971 freeze.
  - Capital credits used +92%, to ₡98.6M, mostly loan- and bond-financed commitments.
  - Payroll +7.7%; basic-education teaching +6.5%; Fuerza Armada +15.6%.
- **Deficit.** Commitment-basis result −₡56.3M (−18.8%), inflated by a new accrual rule for loan-financed projects.
  - **Comparable deficit, excluding ₡36.9M of loan-financed commitments: −₡19.4M (−6.5%),** three times 1970.
  - The Memoria reports a surplus of ₡3.3M by counting loans and bonds as revenue.
- **Liquidity.** Superávit Financiero ₡5.0M, partly from booking ₡49M of loans and bonds receivable. The ₡10M Central Bank advance was repaid and renewed.
- **Debt.** Direct debt +₡32.6M (+24%) to ₡170.2M (57% of revenue). Guarantees ₡190.7M.
- **Composition (gastos):** development 49.8% (+14.5%); security 10.2% (+14.3%). Defensa alone +19.5%.

Currency: US$1.00 = ₡2.50.

## 9. Assessment

**Overall: rising debt, rising stress, high vulnerability (cyclical and political).**

- **The bust followed the boom immediately.** Coffee fell 13.5%, export duties 21%, and revenue missed its estimate by 6.5%.
- **The adjustment was split three ways:**
  - **current spending was frozen** (operating credits used +2.1%), without touching payroll;
  - **investment was financed by debt**: ₡25M of domestic bonds and accelerated foreign loans, mainly for the education reform and roads. Direct debt rose by a quarter;
  - **revenue was raised from consumers**: stamp tax and excises, not coffee or profits.
- **The teachers bore the political cost.** Their 53-day strike was declared illegal and broken by the National Guard, while the Fuerza Armada's budget grew 15.6%, more than twice the rate of basic-education teaching.
- **Transparency worsened.** A new accrual rule let the Memoria report a ₡3.3M surplus in a year with a comparable deficit of about ₡19M.
