# El Salvador: Fiscal Diagnostic and Vulnerability Assessment, 1964

**Technical Note — The Windfall Year: Coffee, the New Income Tax and a Capital Budget Financed with Foreign Loans (November 2026)**

> **About this note.** Fiscal diagnostic for 1964, the third year of the Rivera government. It follows the structure of the diagnostic series for 1952–1963. All figures come from the scanned *Informe Complementario Constitucional* for 1964 (signed 26 February 1965 by Minister Alvaro Marino); 1961–63 comparisons from the revised notes. Every figure was read on the page image and checked by arithmetic.
>
> Pillars: development = education + health + public works; security = Ramo de Defensa, which includes the police (Seguridad Pública).
>
> **Series break.** 1964 introduces a new budget law (D.L. 474), a new income tax law (D. 479) and a new budget structure (D. 478). Spending is split into an operating budget and a capital budget, with new classes. AID programme loans are recorded inside the budget as capital revenue. The Informe does not publish commitments by ramo, so the composition of spending is measured on **payments (gastos)**, and 1963 is recomputed on the same basis.

---

## Executive Summary

**The fiscal position is assessed as "moderate and rising debt, low stress, high vulnerability."** Stress fell sharply: revenue jumped 17%, the budget closed in surplus and the Treasury paid off the Central Bank. The vulnerability is unchanged. Revenue still depends on coffee, and the windfall came from a price recovery (+25%). Meanwhile the government took on ₡52M of undrawn loan commitments, each with counterpart and debt-service obligations.

- **Revenue +16.6%, the largest rise of the series.** Current revenue was ₡210.5M, passing the 1957 peak by 11%. The new income tax added ₡11.0M (+44.5%), the coffee duty ₡8.8M (+40.8%) and consumption taxes ₡7.7M.
- **The income tax is split for the first time:** individuals 50%, companies 40%, other income 11%.
- **Spending rose half as fast as revenue.** Credits used +9.8% to ₡204.6M; payments +8.7% to ₡192.3M.
- **First commitment-basis surplus since 1957:** +₡5.9M (+2.8% of revenue); +₡13.6M excluding spending financed by AID loans.
- **Superávit Financiero ₡13.9M.** The remaining ₡6M of Central Bank notes were paid off.
- **A capital budget of ₡33.5M** (16.4% of credits used): ₡9.2M of direct investment and ₡24.4M of capital transfers. ₡7.75M of AID loans passed through it to rural credit, livestock, irrigation and industrial credit agencies.
- **Composition (gastos):** development 44.1% (45.9% excluding AID-financed spending), security 11.4%, education 22.3%.
- **Direct debt ₡58.9M** (28.0% of revenue), up 20.7% on drawings of ₡16.0M; reported guarantees ₡114.3M on a narrower coverage.

Currency: US$1.00 = ₡2.50, fixed.

**The coffee channel.** The Pink Sheet price rose 25.3% (US$0.81 → US$1.01/kg) and the coffee duty 40.8%. The Memoria attributes the revenue gain also to "the favourable effect of the programming of public works, which generated higher levels of private income" (p. 15).

## 8. Assessment

**Overall: moderate and rising debt, low stress, high vulnerability.** Compared with 1963 ("low-to-moderate stress"), the flow position improved decisively and on its own merits. But the improvement rests on a coffee price recovery, and the government used the year to sign and start drawing foreign loans whose counterpart and service fall on future budgets.

- **Revenue had its best year.** The new income tax and the coffee duty each added about ₡9–11M. Companies' income tax appears for the first time (₡14.2M).
- **The government saved about half the windfall.** Spending rose 9–10%; the financial position rose ₡13.5M and the Central Bank was repaid.
- **The capital budget is born.** Investment now runs through an explicit capital budget of ₡33.5M, three-quarters of it transfers. AID programme loans are inside it; the larger project loans (airport, IDA roads) are still outside.
- **The pillars held.** Education and teachers' salaries rose; security was flat in money and fell as a share.
- **Vulnerability moved to the future.** About ₡52M of undrawn loans and ₡114M of reported guarantees (more on the old coverage) depend on revenue that is again more coffee-dependent.
